Chess
Chess extends Friendship.
The *rules of chess* are the Laws of Chess of FIDE, the International Chess Federation. A *position* is the board with the side to move, the rights to castle and to capture en passant, the positions since the last capture or pawn move and the number of moves made, so that it shows a draw by repetition or by the fifty-move or seventy-five-move rule. The *time limit* of a game is the number of days a player has for each move.
Alice and Bob are persons, Game a game, Move a move, Board and Next positions, Side, Limit and Made numbers, Day, Today and Due dates.
1. ■ Start. Start: Alice and Bob may jointly start Game at Board on Day with Limit days a move, provided Alice and Bob are friends, Board is the first position and the current date is Day. Start as black: Alice and Bob may jointly start Game as black at Board on Day with Limit days a move, provided Alice and Bob are friends, Board is the first position and the current date is Day.
2. ■ Move. Move: Alice may move Game against Bob from Board of Day to Next of Today, provided Alice has the colour Side in Game, Side is the side to move of Board, 1 is the legality of Move on Board, Next is the position after Move on Board, 0 is the automatic draw of Next and the current date is Today. Drawing move: Alice may move Game against Bob to a draw from Board of Day to Next, provided Alice has the colour Side in Game, Side is the side to move of Board, 1 is the legality of Move on Board, Next is the position after Move on Board and 1 is the automatic draw of Next.
3. ■ Win: Alice may claim Game against Bob at Board of Day, provided Bob has the colour Side in Game, Side is the side to move of Board and 1 is the mate of Board.
4. ■ Time: Alice may claim Game against Bob on time at Board of Day, provided Bob has the colour Side in Game, Side is the side to move of Board, Bob has the time limit Limit in Game, Due is Day plus Limit, the current date is Today and Today is more than Due.
5. ■ Resign: Alice may resign Game against Bob at Board of Day.
6. ■ Draw. Draw: Alice and Bob may jointly draw Game at Board of Day, provided Made is the moves made on Board and Made is more than 1. Claim draw: Alice may claim a draw in Game against Bob at Board of Day, provided Alice has the colour Side in Game, Side is the side to move of Board and 1 is the draw of Board.
7. ○ Fair play. Alice will choose each of her moves herself, with no help from a chess engine.
Logic pragmas.
Vocabulary: x plays g against y at b since d = at(x,g,y,b,d); x has the colour s in g = colour(x,g,s); x has the time limit l in g = limit(x,g,l); x has started g = started(x,g); x has won g against y = won(x,g,y); x has drawn g with y = drawn(x,g,y); b is the first position = first(b); v is the legality of m on b = legal(v,m,b); c is the position after m on b = after(c,m,b); s is the side to move of b = side(s,b); v is the mate of b = mate(v,b); v is the draw of b = draw(v,b); v is the automatic draw of b = autodraw(v,b); n is the moves made on b = plies(n,b); the current date is e, for x = today(x,e).
Functions: b is the first position; v is the legality of m on b; c is the position after m on b; s is the side to move of b; v is the mate of b; v is the draw of b; v is the automatic draw of b; n is the moves made on b.
x and y play g at b since d: x plays g against y at b since d and y plays g against x at b since d.
After x and y start g at b on d with l days a move, which requires x has not started g and y has not started g: x has started g, y has started g, x and y play g at b since d, x has the colour 1 in g, y has the colour 2 in g, x has the time limit l in g and y has the time limit l in g.
After x and y start g as black at b on d with l days a move, which requires x has not started g and y has not started g: x has started g, y has started g, x and y play g at b since d, x has the colour 2 in g, y has the colour 1 in g, x has the time limit l in g and y has the time limit l in g.
After x moves g against y from b of d to c of e: no longer x and y play g at b since d and x and y play g at c since e.
After x moves g against y to a draw from b of d to c: no longer x and y play g at b since d, x has drawn g with y and y has drawn g with x.
After x claims g against y at b of d: no longer x and y play g at b since d and x has won g against y.
After x claims g against y on time at b of d: no longer x and y play g at b since d and x has won g against y.
After x resigns g against y at b of d: no longer x and y play g at b since d and y has won g against x.
After x and y draw g at b of d: no longer x and y play g at b since d, x has drawn g with y and y has drawn g with x.
After x claims a draw in g against y at b of d: no longer x and y play g at b since d, x has drawn g with y and y has drawn g with x.
