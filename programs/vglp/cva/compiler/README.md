# The compiler from a contract to its canonical CVA platform

vGLP's compiler, in GLP: `compile(Name, Contract, Verdict, Intro, Pragmas)` prints the canonical CVA platform of an act-schema contract in vGLP (vGLP, Section 5.4, "The compilation", "Act schemas", "Act schemas as local specifications" and "The contract's words"), between the lines `%% compile begin` and `%% compile end`, or the lines saying why it does not.  `compile/4`, with no pragmas, and `compile/3`, with no introduction predicate either, remain.  The modules: `cvac.glp`, what is printed from the contract; `text.glp`, the text every platform shares; `local.glp`, whether the contract is a local and effective specification; `self.glp`, the types; `pragmas.sh`, the reader of a contract's pragmas.  The scripts that run it are `programs/vglp/cva/compile.sh`, `programs/apps/compile.sh`, `programs/cssn/compile.sh` (CSSN's) and `cascade.sh`.

## The forms the papers leave open

vGLP's paper says what the platform does and what the pragmas carry (Section 5.4, "The compilation" and "The contract's words"; Section 6.2, "The words a platform shows"), and GSC's paper says how a contract writes its pragmas (Section 2, its first paragraph).  The papers name these things and not all their term forms; the forms below are the compiler's, for vGLP to put in its paper.

1. **How a pragma line becomes a term the compiler reads.**  `pragmas.sh <NAME.txt>` reads the lines after the line `Pragmas` of the contract's `NAME.txt`, and of the `NAME.txt` of every contract its `% Extends:` line names, each contract extended first, an owner named in the possessive, `GSC's`, not a contract, and prints one term, `pragmas(Lists, Acts)`, or `no_pragmas` where the contract's own `NAME.txt` has no line `Pragmas`.  The lines after `Pragmas` up to the first that names a clause are lists, one for each tab of the app (GSC, Section 2, its first paragraph, as GSC ruled, Code_inbox.md, 2026-10-24 09:40 UTC), as GB's `Coins: the coins Alice holds.` and `Bonds: the bonds Alice holds.`: `Contacts: the persons Alice knows.` is `list("Contacts", [the, persons, person, alice, knows, know])`, the words of what it lists in lower case, each also without a final s.  A contract's names are those its declaration line gives, after its title and before clause 1, "Alice, Bob and Carol are persons." (GKK.txt at GLP ec7c578c), the declaration being part of the contract the reader takes; a contract with no declaration has as its names the words of a capital and lower-case letters its clauses write within a sentence, not first in it.  Every other line names a clause, in GSC's form (GSC 25b6b3c, Section 2, its first paragraph; GSC's guide, `docs/act-schemas-guide.md`, step 7): the line names the proposer, then a button for each act they propose and any field they fill, `Meeting: Alice [Meet] <person>`, and each line after it indented two spaces names another proposer with their buttons, `  Alice [Close]`, or a person asked, a colon and the question, `  Bob: Alice wishes that you know each other.`, a question line belonging to the nearest button line above it.  "A name in a pragma is the schema's role of that name, the schemas naming their roles as the clauses do" (GSC 25b6b3c, Section 2): the reader maps each name to the role the schema term names so, `schema(Name, Names, Vars, Roles)` (programs/gsc/README.md), and a schema term with no `Names` is a fault, the contract not compiled; a name in a question that no role of the schema bears is the agent variable of an act with one, as GKK's Carol is introduce's `c`, filled by its field, and any other is a fault.  The proposer must be in a guarding role, each other guarding role of the act must be asked, and the proposer is not asked but in an act of one guarding role, whose question is text of its request form.  A line whose buttons come first, naming no proposer, is a fault ("A button is offered only to the person named before it", vGLP 8aa82f0, Section 5.4).  Either line gives an act for each of its buttons: `Meeting: [Meet] Alice wishes that you know each other.` is `act(meet, "Meet", ["person"], said([[], [person(1), text(" wishes that you know each other.")]]), "Contacts", by([1]))`: the schema the button names, the button in brackets, the fields in angle brackets in order, the questions, one for each guarding role in GSC's form, the proposer's `[]`, the name of the tab of its contract, the first tab of the pragmas of the `NAME.txt` the line is in, and the guarding roles at which the act is proposed, `by(Gs)`, each one whose every other guarding role has a question (vGLP 3ec07a2, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A guarding role with no question is never asked, so an act is proposed only at a guarding role whose every other guarding role has a question"), the line's one question in the other form being put to each guarding role but the first and its questions one per guarding role to each; a guarded act with no such role is a fault, and the contract is not compiled; the title before the colon must be a clause's of the same `NAME.txt`.  A string is carried as its GLP source text inside a quoted name, `'"Meet"'`, since the compiler's printer writes a constant as it is and GLP has no string concatenation.

2. **Which schema a pragma's act is.**  A line has a button for each act of its clause a person proposes, and each act is the schema whose name is its button's text in lower case, each space an underscore (GSC, Section 2: "a button for each act of it a person proposes, in brackets, each bearing its act's name"): `[Establish]` the schema `establish`, `[Post own]` the schema `post_own`; the line's fields and its questions are each of its acts'.  The button names also the schemas bearing its name with a suffix after an underscore, as GB's `[Swap]` names `swap_cc`, `swap_cb`, `swap_bc` and `swap_bb` and CSSN's `[Enter]` `enter` and `enter_2`, but for a schema another button of the contract names by its own name, as `[Post own]` names `post_own` and `[Post]` does not (GSC #7, GSC's docs/design-decisions.md, 2026-10-24: "the schemas realising it are those bearing its name or its name with a suffix after an underscore"), and its line gives an act for each; their fields are the union of their fields as `field_places` counts them, the peer of an acquaintance schema and then the variables of the head a person fills, in head order, each variable merged in after the one before it in its own schema, the line's fields naming the union's first variables in order, and each schema's act carries the names of its own variables, in its order, an inapplicable field left empty and none after its last named variable (GSC's ruling, Code_inbox.md, 2026-10-24 08:06 UTC).  The variables are those the request names: a numeric variable local to a role, one that occurs only in atoms the schema requires or deletes at that one role, and in the constraint, is bound by that role's state, and no field names it (vGLP, Section 5.4, "Act schemas as local specifications": "Every other numeric variable is bound by the schema-binding, as a speech-act variable is, and a request names it, so the request form takes it"), as `t`, the issuer's date, in GB's `redeem_bc` and `redeem_bb`, so that `[Redeem] <maturity given> <issuer wanted> <maturity wanted>` names `d`, `r` and `d1`.  `pragmas.sh <NAME.txt> <contract term>` reads the schemas and their variables from the term, which the scripts pass it.  A button that names no schema of the contract, by either reading, is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.  A guarded schema, one the request type proposes, that no button names is a fault, and the contract is not compiled, the platform's line saying which, `%% a guarded schema has no button in the contract's pragmas:(S)` (vGLP 932c710, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A guarded schema is proposed by a person, by its button, so the compiler requires a button for every guarded schema; an unguarded one is proposed by its opener's machine and has none").

3. **Which predicate a list lists.**  The predicate named by its name, an underscore read as a space, the longest such phrase of the line, a word also read without a final s (vGLP 110caf4, Section 5.4, "The contract's words"): `the persons Alice knows` lists `knows`, `the friends of Alice` `friend`, `the groups Alice is a member of` `member`, `those Alice is principal of` `principal_of`.  A list naming none, or more than one of that length, is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.

4. **The view.**  The view the agent writes is `view(S, K, A)`: the machine state, the persons the agent knows, and the approvals its person made that are pending, `Approved ::= Proposal`, each by its request name, a request's own and an answer's that of its attempt's part, oldest first, so that no attempt and no list is in it (vGLP e286276, Section 5.4, "The compilation"); a contract with no guarded part has no approvals, its `Approved` being `Constant`; with pragmas, `view(S, K, A, Ts)`, `Ts` the lists, each `tab(Name, P)`, its name and the predicate whose atoms in `S` it lists, `Tab ::= tab(String, Constant)`.  The list holds the predicate and not the atoms, so that no list is under another, as Definition "Volitional Procedure, Interactive Type" requires of the output.  A view is written when the agent starts and whenever the machine state, the persons known or the approvals pending change.

5. **How a question's names map to the schema's persons.**  By the names the schema term gives its roles (1).  The platform writes the question into the approval question, `approval(Attempt, List(Word), YesNo?)`, `Word ::= text(String) ; person(Peer) ; named(String, Peer) ; valued(String, Integer)`, each person the one the attempt's schema-binding puts in that place (`sentence_of/3`, printed per schema, its question of each role `q(R, Words)`, `ask_of/3` giving that of the role the agent is in); after its sentence the question shows each value of the binding it does not name, by the name of its field, `named(F, P)` a person and `valued(F, K)` a number (vGLP 110caf4, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "and shows after it each value of the binding the question does not name, by the name of its field, so that the person sees the whole proposal"), the k-th field of the schema's act naming the k-th place `field_places` counts, the peer of an acquaintance schema first, and a field left empty, an inapplicable field of a schema that shares its button, naming none.

6. **What the widget declaration of the request type carries.**  The compiler prints two widget declarations into every platform with a guarded part, `Approval =::= approval_sentence.` and `Menu =::= social_menu(...)`, and the first alone into one with none, which has no request question.  With pragmas, the request type's carries, for each schema the request type proposes:
   - `label(F, S)`, the request whose name is a schema-binding of `F`, labelled with the button `S`;
   - `label(F, I, S)`, its `I`-th place, a field the person fills, named `S`: the fields in angle brackets name, in order, the peer of an acquaintance schema, a person the act names by key, and then the variables of the schema's head after its roles (vGLP e286276, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "in the order of the schema's variables after its roles, a person the act names by key among them"), as GC's `swap(p?, q?; u, k, v, k')` has `<issuer given> <amount given> <issuer wanted> <amount wanted>`, GSN's `post(p?, a; x, n)` its one field `<text>`, `x`, GKK's `introduce(p?, q?; c)` its field `<person>`, `c`, and GKK's `meet(p?, q?)` its field `<person>`, the peer `q`; a field is drawn by its name, and none by the name of its type;
   - `act(F, T, M, Ps, Gs, Is)`, one for each guarding role at which the act is proposed, `by(Gs)` of its pragma (vGLP 3ec07a2, Section 6.2: "The request widget offers an act only where the person's machine can propose it, and at a guarding role at which the act is proposed"), its place `M` (vGLP 110caf4, Section 6.2: "the button of an act is drawn at each of its guarding roles, beside each row that fills its variable"), where its button is drawn (vGLP 932c710, Section 6.2: "The button of an act that names, beside the person proposing it, a person, a group or an item is drawn beside each one the view lists, on whichever tab lists it, the row filling the act's variable, and the button of an act that names none on the tab of its contract"): `T` the tab of its contract; `M` the place of the person proposing it, its first guarding role; `Gs` the groups it names, `group(J, K)`, for each name term `<u>.y` of its atoms, `J` the place of the role `u` and `K` that of the speech-act variable `y`, which no field names; `Ps` the places of the persons it names beside the proposer that no field names and that lead no group it names; and `Is` the items it names, `item(K, P)`, for each atom of a predicate `P` a list of the app lists, kept or deleted at the proposer's role, with a speech-act variable, a person of another role or an agent variable at place `K` among its arguments, so that a row of that list fills it, as GSNP's `forward(p?, q ; x)`, requiring `item(x)` at `p`, has `item(3, item)`, and a field naming that variable labels it;
     and, after them, the act's precondition at that guarding role (vGLP feda043, Section 6.2, "The words a platform shows": "where the viewer's state holds the atoms the act requires at the viewer's role and lacks those it forbids there"): `req(P, As)` for each atom the schema requires or deletes there and `forb(P, As)` for each it forbids, `As` each argument's place, `at(K)`, a group's, `grp(J, K)`, the places of its leader and its name, or `any`, a variable no place names, as a numeric variable local to the role; an atom of a reflexive predicate naming that role itself is left out, holding at every person of that person.  The construct family draws the button of an act that names one person, group or item beside the proposer beside a row of a tab only where some `req` of the tab's predicate names the place the row fills and the viewer's state holds every `req`, a place not filled matching any argument, and lacks every `forb` whose places are all filled; an act that names two or more, a person a field names by key among them, is a button at the top of the tab of its contract, one for each label, which opens its form, the proposer at the first guarding role whose `req` of the tab's predicate and whole precondition the viewer's state meets, or the first whose precondition it meets, or the first;
     and `unless(P, As)`, what the schemas prove (vGLP 893d4ea, Section 6.2: "where every schema that adds an atom naming x at a role requires or adds at x's role an atom that no schema deletes, the viewer holding the first shows that x holds the second, and an act that forbids the second at x's role is not drawn beside x"): when the contract is compiled, for each predicate P, its arity and an argument naming another role, the atoms that every schema adding P there requires or adds at that role whose predicate no schema deletes, their arguments that role or the viewer's; and, for an act at the viewer's role forbidding such an atom at the role of its place K, `unless(P, As)`, `As` `at(K)` at that argument and `any` elsewhere, the button not drawn where the viewer's state holds a matching atom;
   - `meeting(F, I)`, `F` an acquaintance schema, its peer at place `I`, which a field for the key fills where the peer is not listed (Section 6.2: "Meet with a peer not listed takes a field for the key");
   - `text(F, Ws)`, the question of an act with one guarding role, text of its request form, each word `say(S)` or `at(I)`, the person at place `I`.

   The canonical compilation (`glp_runtime/lib/vglp`, `canonical.dart`, `DeclaredWidget`, `ActPlace`; `constructs.dart`, `_labelled`) writes each label beside its constructor in the widget term: `label(S, W)` around the widget `W` of the labelled constructor, and `label(S, C)` for a constant `C` in a row of buttons; a field named by the declaration is entered `field(S, W)`; and the places of a request around its labelled widget, `act(place(T, M, Ps, Gs, Is, Ks, Ws), W)`, the root's type `Widget` (programs/self.glp) carrying `act(ActPlace, Widget)`.  A constructor with no label is written as before, and the construct family draws the name of its type (Section 6.2: "A construct family draws the label it is given, and the name of the type where it is given none"); a construct family that does not place buttons draws `W`.

7. **An approval question's two answers.**  The pragmas give none, so the approval type's declaration carries no label, and the construct family draws the two constants, `yes` and `no`, two of the platform's four words.

8. **Acquaintance.**  An acquaintance schema is one of two party roles, both guarding, whose atoms required or deleted at each role name neither the other role nor a name term built on it (vGLP, Section 5.4, "The compilation": "for a contract the parts of an acquaintance schema, one with two roles, both guarding, that requires at neither role an atom naming the other, as GKK's meet").  `meeting_peer(N, Me, P)`, printed per acquaintance schema, gives the peer of a request name `N` beside the agent `Me`, `peer(Q)` or `nopeer`.  A hello carries the request name, `hello(P, N)`; the approval question it asks is `approval(hello(Q, N), Ws, YesNo?)`, `Asking ::= Attempt ; hello(Peer, Proposal)`, asked by `greet`, the person's yes their request `propose(N)`, read by a trigger of its own, `greeted(N)`, as a request is read, its hello sent back, and no next request question asked, the yes answering no request question.  In vGLP's own contracts (`../contracts.glp`), whose befriend requires nothing of the other role, befriend is an acquaintance schema, and their plays come to know each other by it.

9. **A request of the person held.**  A request is taken where the agent guards and is ready for the part it names (Section 5.4: "the Request of {[t]} to itself for each class [t] of a guarded part with no opener named N that it guards and is ready for").  A request read while the agent is not free for a class of its Request is held, the agent state's last field, and put up again, `again(N)`, after every dispatch that changes the app state or consumes a start, after which alone whether the agent is free can change; the held requests are put up before the held starts, so that a request held while the agent was busy is an approval again before a start of its class is taken, and its person is not asked on that start (vGLP #20, item 1: "so no second question is asked"); it is dropped where the agent is free and no Request is enabled, with the notice `refused(N, p, unable)` where the agent is ready for no part named `N` that it guards or opens (Section 5.4: "with a notice refused(N, p, unable)"); every notice names the request name of the refused attempt's part, `refused(N, q, d)` (vGLP e286276, Section 5.4); `Reason ::= declined ; unable ; unable(List(Atom), List(Atom))`.

10. **The day.**  A contract requires the day where a schema requires `today(d)` at a role.  Its agent takes a fifth argument, the calendar, `Stream(Integer)?`, merged with its triggers as `day(D)`; on each date it replaces `today(d)` in its state and puts up again the starts and the requests it holds.  The agent holds `today(d)` as an atom of the machine state of its app state, which no schema adds or deletes; `atom_of` does not show it, the type `Atom` has no alternative for it, and `show_state` does not compare it, so the view never shows it (Section 5.4, "Act schemas as local specifications": "holds today(d), d the last date read, beside its app state").

11. **Constraints.**  A schema's constraint is read in GSC's term form (`programs/gsc/self.glp`): `true`, `and(C1, C2)`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge` and `gt` of two expressions, `succ(X, Y)` and `or(C1, C2)`; an expression a numeric argument, `plus(X, Y)`, `minus(X, Y)` or `mul(X, Y)` (GSC, Definition "Schema with values").  The values are whole numbers, `Integer`.  An equality gives its one numeric variable that a binding leaves free the whole number at which its two sides are equal, where its coefficient is not 0 and there is one; a conjunction what either conjunct gives, taken again with each value given; an inequality gives none (vGLP, Section 5.4: "or the constraint gives the variable finitely many values once those are bound").

A contract with no pragmas, as vGLP's own (`../contracts.glp`), is compiled as before, but for the two widget declarations.
