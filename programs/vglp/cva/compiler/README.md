# The compiler from a contract to its canonical CVA platform

vGLP's compiler, in GLP: `compile(Name, Contract, Verdict, Intro, Pragmas)` prints the canonical CVA platform of an act-schema contract in vGLP (vGLP, Section 5.4, "The compilation", "Act schemas", "Act schemas as local specifications" and "The contract's words"), between the lines `%% compile begin` and `%% compile end`, or the lines saying why it does not.  `compile/4`, with no pragmas, and `compile/3`, with no introduction predicate either, remain, and check a contract without printing a platform: "the compiler prints a platform only from a contract with pragmas, and refuses one with none; the checks that a contract is local, effective and grassroots are of its schemas and need none" (vGLP ffcb093, Section 5.4, "The contract's words"), so a contract that passes the checks with no pragmas is refused with the line `%% not compiled, having no pragmas(Name)`.  vGLP's own contracts, `../contracts.glp`, carry their pragmas as a term beside them, `pragmas_named/2`, which `../compile.sh` gives the compiler.  The modules: `cvac.glp`, what is printed from the contract; `text.glp`, the text every platform shares; `local.glp`, whether the contract is a local and effective specification; `self.glp`, the types and the procedures that build a line; `pragmas.sh`, the reader of a contract's pragmas.  The scripts that run it are `programs/vglp/cva/compile.sh`, `programs/apps/compile.sh`, `programs/cssn/compile.sh` (CSSN's) and `cascade.sh`.

## The forms the papers leave open

vGLP's paper says what the platform does and what the pragmas carry (Section 5.4, "The compilation" and "The contract's words"; Section 6.2, "The words a platform shows"), and GSC's paper says how a contract writes its pragmas (Section 2, its first paragraph).  The papers name these things and not all their term forms; the forms below are the compiler's, for vGLP to put in its paper.

1. **How a pragma line becomes a term the compiler reads.**  `pragmas.sh <NAME.txt>` reads the lines after the line `Pragmas`, or `Interface pragmas`, of the contract's `NAME.txt`, skipping those after `Implementation pragmas` up to the next interface heading, no paper yet saying what form the implementation pragmas take in the app's code (GSC, sections/02-contracts.tex:3; GSA, sections/s3-super-app.tex:69; GSC #13, Code_inbox.md, 2026-11-07 19:29 UTC, 2), and of the `NAME.txt` of every contract its `% Extends:` line names, each contract extended first, an owner named in the possessive, `GSC's`, not a contract, and a contract's name read whole with a hyphened part, as GFF's `GDO-L`, and prints one term, `pragmas(Lists, Acts)`, or `no_pragmas` where the contract's own `NAME.txt` has no line `Pragmas`, which the compiler refuses.  A tab of an inherited tab's name replaces it (vGLP 14d9064, Section 5.4, "The contract's words", its last sentence: "A contract that extends or amends others has their tabs with its own, a tab of an inherited tab's name replacing it"): the tab of the contract extending stands in the place of the inherited one, which is gone, as GDO-L's `Pointing` replaces GDO's and DGC's `Coins` those of GB and GC (Section VG, replace_tab).  An inherited tab stands while the contract has the predicate it lists (vGLP e1d1b8a, Section 5.4, the same sentence: "an inherited tab standing while the contract has the predicate it lists, as an inherited button stands while the contract has an act it names"): one naming no predicate of the contract's schemas is gone, with the line `<NAME>: the tab <Title>: gone, the contract having no predicate it lists`, as GSN's Messages and Posts are in GSNP, whose items are GFP's; a tab of the contract's own that names none stands, naming none, so that the fault shows (Section VG, drop_tab).  A button is of the acts it names, and a button replaces a button, matched by name and not by clause title (vGLP 14d9064, Section 5.4, "The contract's words", its last sentence: "and the buttons of the acts it has: an inherited button, with its fields and its questions, stands while the contract has an act it names, an act an amendment deletes being gone and its button with it; and where the contract writes a button of an inherited button's name, its button, with its fields and its questions, replaces the inherited one, the other buttons of the inherited line standing"): a button, with its fields, is replaced where a contract extending its contract writes a button of its name, the other buttons of its line standing with the line's questions, and a line all of whose buttons are replaced is gone with its questions, as XK's [Close] leaves the [Create] of XG's line [Create] <name> [Close] (Section VG, replace_one), as DGC's Minting line replaces GB's and GD's Friendship line GSG's; and an inherited button whose contract term has no act it names goes, with its fields, as GC's `[Mint]` in DGC.  The contracts a contract has the pragmas of are those named on the line saying what it is written from, the first after its title and before its names declaration, `<NAME> <relation> <OTHER> (...).` or with no parentheses, in GSC's form (GSC df5e454, Section 2: "each relation is followed by the contract it is to, or by `it' for the contract named last, the relations joined by `and', and an instantiation says after the contract's name, between commas, what it writes for what, as ``CSSN instantiates GSNW, a child for a ward and a parent for a guardian, and restricts and extends it''"): the relation one of `amends`, `extends`, `instantiates`, `implements` and `restricts`, relations sharing their contract joined by `and`, as `restricts and extends it`, several contracts of one relation joined by commas and `and`, as `GSNP amends GSN and GFP, and GAI.`, an instantiation's words between commas after its contract, naming none, which it may omit, as `MCS instantiates GBank and amends it.` (GSC 30013e6, Section 2: "an instantiation may say after the contract's name, between commas, what it writes for what"), and a name read whole with a hyphened part (style guide 36; GSC, Section 8; vGLP #23, Code_inbox.md, 2026-11-06 07:17 UTC, 1; Code #27, Cloud-vGLP_inbox.md, 2026-11-10 18:04 UTC, 2); `pragmas.sh --from <NAME.txt>` prints the `NAME.txt` of each contract it is written from, transitively, the contract itself last, and where a contract has no such line the contracts its `% Extends:` line names, until Coordination announces the owners have moved; `compile.sh`, `programs/apps/compile.sh`, `programs/cssn/compile.sh` and `cascade.sh` read it through `pragmas.sh`.  The lines after `Pragmas` up to the first that names a clause are lists, one for each tab of the app (GSC, Section 2, its first paragraph, as GSC ruled, Code_inbox.md, 2026-10-24 09:40 UTC), as GB's `Coins: the coins Alice holds.` and `Bonds: the bonds Alice holds.`: `Contacts: the persons Alice knows.` is `list("Contacts", [the, persons, person, alice, knows, know])`, the words of what it lists in lower case, each also without a final s.  A contract's names are those its declaration line gives, after its title and before clause 1, "Alice, Bob and Carol are persons." (Knowledge.txt at GLP ec7c578c), every name its sentence lists, of every kind, as GnW's "Alice, Bob and Erin are persons, Carol and Dave wards." gives five (vGLP 722343d, Section 5.4: "a name the contract's declaration gives a person or a ward"; GSC, Section 2: "a declaration of the contract's names, saying what each names"), the declaration being part of the contract the reader takes; a contract with no declaration has as its names the words of a capital and lower-case letters its clauses write within a sentence, not first in it.  Every other line names a clause, in GSC's form (GSC 25b6b3c, Section 2, its first paragraph; GSC's guide, `docs/speech-act-schemas-guide.md`, step 7): the line names the proposer, then a button for each act they propose and any field they fill, `Acquaintance: Alice [Acquaint] <person>`, and each line after it indented two spaces names another proposer with their buttons, `  Alice [Close]`, or a person asked, a colon and the question, `  Bob: Alice wishes that you know each other.`, a question line belonging to the nearest button line above it.  "A name in a pragma is the schema's role of that name, the schemas naming their roles as the clauses do" (GSC 25b6b3c, Section 2): the reader maps each name to the role the schema term names so, `schema(Name, Names, Vars, Roles)` (programs/gsc/README.md), and a schema term with no `Names` is a fault, the contract not compiled; a name in a question that no role of the schema bears is the agent variable of an act with one, as GKK's Carol is introduce's `c`, filled by its field, and any other is a fault.  The proposer must be in a guarding role, each other guarding role of the act must be asked, and the proposer is not asked but in an act of one guarding role, whose question is text of its request form.  A line whose buttons come first, naming no proposer, is a fault ("A button is offered only to the person named before it", vGLP 8aa82f0, Section 5.4).  Either line gives an act for each of its buttons: `Acquaintance: Alice [Acquaint] <person>` with its question line `  Bob: Alice wishes that you know each other.` (Knowledge.txt) is `act(acquaint, "Acquaint", ["person"], said([[], [person(1), text(" wishes that you know each other.")]]), "Contacts", by([1]))`: the schema the button names, the button in brackets, the fields in angle brackets in order, the questions, one for each guarding role in GSC's form, the proposer's `[]`, the name of the tab of its contract, the first tab of the pragmas of the `NAME.txt` the line is in, and the guarding roles at which the act is proposed, `by(Gs)`, each one whose every other guarding role has a question (vGLP 3ec07a2, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A guarding role with no question is never asked, so an act is proposed only at a guarding role whose every other guarding role has a question"), the line's one question in the other form being put to each guarding role but the first and its questions one per guarding role to each; a guarded act with no such role is a fault, and the contract is not compiled; the title before the colon must be a clause's of the same `NAME.txt`.  A string is carried as its GLP source text inside a quoted name, `'"Acquaint"'`, since the compiler's printer writes a constant as it is and GLP has no string concatenation.

2. **Which schema a pragma's act is.**  A line has a button for each act of its clause a person proposes, and each act is the schema whose name is its button's text in lower case, each space an underscore, and every character that is no letter, digit, underscore or hyphen left out (`[Redeem member's coin]` the schema `redeem_members_coin`) (GSC, Section 2: "a button for each act of it a person proposes, in brackets, each bearing its act's name"): `[Establish]` the schema `establish`, `[Post own]` the schema `post_own`; the line's questions are each of its acts', and the fields after a button, up to the next button, are that button's, each button checked against its own fields (vGLP 40f8444, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "for each act they propose, its button, in brackets, bearing the act's name, and its fields, in angle brackets"; vGLP #23, Code_inbox.md, 2026-11-05 13:33 UTC, 2), as DGC's `[Mint coin] <denomination> <amount> [Mint bond] <denomination> <maturity> <amount>` and GSN's `[Create] <name> [Close]`.  A button is of the acts it names, so one name is one button, with one line: two lines that stand writing a button of one name, or one line writing it twice, are a fault of the contract, refused, the button and both lines named (ibid.: "A button is of the acts it names"; vGLP #23, 1).  The button names also the schemas bearing its name with a suffix after an underscore, as GB's `[Swap]` names `swap_cc`, `swap_cb`, `swap_bc` and `swap_bb` and CSSN's `[Enter]` `enter` and `enter_2`, but for a schema another button of the contract names by its own name, as `[Post own]` names `post_own` and `[Post]` does not (GSC #7, GSC's docs/design-decisions.md, 2026-10-24: "the schemas realising it are those bearing its name or its name with a suffix after an underscore"), and its line gives an act for each; the fields of the line name the variables of the acts its button names together, in the schemas' order, each variable merged in after the one before it in its own schema (GSC's ruling, Code_inbox.md, 2026-10-24 08:06 UTC), and each schema's act carries the names of its own variables, in its order, an inapplicable field left empty and none after its last named variable.  The variables a field names are those the atoms the schema requires at the proposer's role do not bind (vGLP 40f8444, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A field is for a variable of the schema that those atoms do not bind --- a text, a number, a new name, a person the proposer's state does not name --- and the fields of a line name these variables, of the acts its button names together, in the schemas' order; the compiler refuses a line whose fields are not these"): of the places of its request a field may name, its roles but its listed roles and then the variables of its head, those that no atom required or deleted at the proposer's role names, a deleted atom being required and a cleared one requiring nothing, a role named by `role(K)` or by `own(K)` in a name term, a speech-act variable `y` by a name term `N.y`, and a multiplicity, `times(K, A)`, naming no variable of `K`.  A variable local to a role, agent, speech-act or numeric, one that occurs only in atoms the schema requires or deletes at that one role, and in the constraint, inside a name term or a signed term included, is bound by that role's state, is no place of the request and has no field (vGLP 8a3ae79, Section 5.4, "Speech-act schemas as local specifications": "A schema-binding does not bind a local variable: the machine of the person in that role finds a value for it in its own state.  Every other variable is bound by the schema-binding, and a request names it"), as `t`, the issuer's date, in GB's `redeem_bc` and `redeem_bb`, and `d`, the person Bob points to with the label, in GDO-L's `lpoint_friend` (Section VG, local_field and localagent); its machine votes yes where its state holds the required atoms for some value of it, and takes the effect at one value where the schema deletes an atom it occurs in (`dom_bindings`, `apply_with`), and the widget draws one button for the ways that differ only in it, the `req` of its atoms carrying `any` at its place.  `pragmas.sh` refuses a line with fewer fields than these variables, naming the variable unnamed, and one with more, naming the field beyond them and the variables the proposer's atoms bind; with `PRAGMAS_LINES` set it prints each button line and the variables its fields name by the rule, or why it is refused, replaced or gone.  `pragmas.sh <NAME.txt> <contract term>` reads the schemas and their variables from the term, which the scripts pass it as GSC's reader reads it, `as_read/2` of `programs/gsc`, each schema of the clause syntax, `sas/7`, in the checker's form with its roles' names; the compiler takes the same reading, and neither keeps a copy of the reader (vGLP, Section 5.4, "Speech-act schemas"; vGLP #23, Code_inbox.md, 2026-11-05 14:20 UTC, 1).  A button that names no schema of the contract, by either reading, is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.  A guarded schema, one the request type proposes, that no button names is a fault, and the contract is not compiled, the platform's line saying which, `%% a guarded schema has no button in the contract's pragmas:(S)` (vGLP 932c710, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A guarded schema is proposed by a person, by its button, so the compiler requires a button for every guarded schema; an unguarded one is proposed by its opener's machine and has none").

3. **Which predicate a list lists.**  The predicate named by its name, an underscore read as a space, the longest such phrase of the line, a word also read without a final s (vGLP 110caf4, Section 5.4, "The contract's words"): `the persons Alice knows` lists `knows`, `the friends of Alice` `friend`, `the groups Alice is a member of` `member`, `those Alice is principal of` `principal_of`.  A list naming none, or more than one of that length, is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.

4. **The view.**  The view the agent writes is `view(Id, S, K, A, Ts)`: the agent's own key, the machine state, the persons the agent knows, and the approvals its person made that are pending, `Approved ::= Proposal`, each by its request name, a request's own and an answer's that of its attempt's part, oldest first, so that no attempt and no list is in it (vGLP e286276, Section 5.4, "The compilation"); a contract with no guarded part has no approvals, its `Approved` being `Constant`; `Ts` the lists, last in the view (vGLP 12f629d, Section 5.4, "The contract's words"), each `tab(Name, P)`, its name and the predicate whose atoms in `S` it lists, `Tab ::= tab(String, Constant)`.  The list holds the predicate and not the atoms, so that no list is under another, as Definition "Volitional Procedure, Interactive Type" requires of the output.  The first view is `view(Id?, [], [], [], Ts?)`.  The request question is bare, `procedure (Request?)*request(Stream(Trigger))`, its question of the request type alone; the request form reads the key and the persons known from the last view drawn on construct 0 (Section 6.2).  A view is written when the agent starts and whenever the machine state, the persons known or the approvals pending change.

5. **How a question's names map to the schema's persons.**  By the names the schema term gives its roles (1).  The platform writes the question into the approval question, `approval(Attempt, List(Word), YesNo?)`, `Word ::= text(String) ; person(Peer) ; named(String, Peer) ; valued(String, Number) ; held(Atom, Integer)`, each person the one the attempt's schema-binding puts in that place (`sentence_of/3`, printed per schema, its question of each role `q(R, G, Words)`, `G` the role of the proposer, the first at which the act is proposed other than `R`'s, `ask_of/3` giving `asked(G, Words)`, that of the role the agent is in, and `asked(0, [])` where it is in none); after its sentence the question shows each value of the binding it does not name, by the name of its field, `named(F, P)` a person and `valued(F, K)` a number (vGLP 110caf4, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "and shows after it each value of the binding the question does not name, by the name of its field, so that the person sees the whole proposal"), the k-th field of the schema's act naming the k-th place `field_places` counts, the peer of an acquaintance schema first, and a field left empty, an inapplicable field of a schema that shares its button, naming none.  After the values it shows "the atoms the act requires of the proposer whose predicates the tabs list, as the tabs draw them --- in a swap, the coins the proposer gives" (vGLP e22e2ad, Section 5.4, "The contract's words"): `question_words/3` of the printed text adds to what `sentence_of` gives, for the role `G`, one word `held(A, K)` for each atom the schema requires there, its predicate listed by a tab, under the binding the attempt's part names, `K` its copies; each atom a word of its own, so that no list is under another (4).  An atom naming a variable local to the proposer's role is under no binding of the attempt ("A schema-binding does not bind a local variable", ibid., "Speech-act schemas as local specifications"), and is left out; so is an atom of a reflexive predicate naming the proposer, which no tab lists.

6. **What the widget declaration of the request type carries.**  The compiler prints two widget declarations into every platform, `Approval =::= approval_sentence.` and `Request? =::= social_menu(Buttons)`, each on the moded type of its question, `(Approval)*respond` and `(Approval)*greet` the one, `(Request?)*request` the other (vGLP, Section 6.2, Definition "Widget Declaration": "where $T$ is a moded type"), a platform with no guarded part having no request question and only the first (16).  With pragmas, the request type's declaration carries the buttons and the fields of the contract's acts (vGLP 4236349, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "the buttons and the fields into the widget declaration it prints for the platform's request type"; Section 6.2: "for its request type, a menu of the buttons and the fields of the contract's acts"), for each button its label, the acts it names, and for each act the atoms its schema requires and forbids at the proposer's role, the places of its request those atoms bind, and its fields, each with its label and its type, in the schema's order (Section 6.2: the request widget "draws one button for each way the viewer's state, in the last view written, satisfies the act at the proposer's role, with the values that way binds and the act's fields").  It carries no tab, no row and no item: where the construct family draws a button is its own choice, from the atoms that bind the way (Section 6.2: "How the buttons of one act are arranged, each beside the atoms that bind it or as one button with a picker, is the construct family's").  Its exact grammar, every constant written as GLP writes it and every string as a double-quoted string:

   ```
   Request? =::= social_menu(Buttons).
   Buttons   ::= [] ; [Button | Buttons].
   Button    ::= button(String, Acts).
   Acts      ::= [] ; [Act | Acts].
   Act       ::= act(Constant, Integer, Ints, Preconds, Ints, Fields, Preconds, Words).
   Preconds  ::= [] ; [Precond | Preconds].
   Precond   ::= req(Constant, CondArgs) ; forb(Constant, CondArgs)
               ; unless(Constant, CondArgs) ; proved(Constant, Preds, Constant, CondArgs)
               ; same(Integer, Integer).
   CondArgs  ::= [] ; [CondArg | CondArgs].
   CondArg   ::= at(Integer) ; grp(Integer, Integer) ; any.
   Preds     ::= [] ; [Constant | Preds].
   Ints      ::= [] ; [Integer | Ints].
   Fields    ::= [] ; [Field | Fields].
   Field     ::= field(Integer, String, FieldType).
   FieldType ::= person ; key ; agent ; text ; number.
   Words     ::= [] ; [Word | Words].
   Word      ::= say(String) ; at(Integer).
   ```

   - `button(B, Acts)`, one for each button of the pragmas, in their order, `B` its label in brackets and `Acts` the acts it names that the request type proposes, a button naming none of them left out; one name is one button (Section 5.4: "A button is of the acts it names"), so the acts of a button that names several schemas, as GB's `[Swap]` names `swap_cc`, `swap_cb`, `swap_bc` and `swap_bb`, are in one `button`, and the construct family draws it once for the ways of its acts that bind the same values (Section 6.2: "A button that names several acts is drawn once for the ways of its acts that bind the same values").
   - `act(F, M, Roles, Pre, Binds, Fields, Same, Text)`, one for each guarding role at which the act is proposed, `by(Gs)` of its pragma (1): `F` the schema, the functor of its request name `propose(F(...))`, a place `I` of the request being the `I`-th argument of `F(...)`, the schema's head less its listed roles (4, 5); `M` the place of the proposer, the viewer; `Roles` the places of the request that hold roles, `M` among them.  A way is a schema-binding: "every binding of the variables of the atoms the schema requires there that a schema-binding makes, distinct roles bound to distinct persons" (vGLP e22e2ad, Section 5.4, "The contract's words"), and "distinct roles are bound to distinct agents, unless the clause says of two names 'possibly one person'" (ibid., "Speech-act schemas as local specifications").  So the widget offers no way and no field value that binds two places of `Roles` to one person, but for a pair of `Same`, and an agent variable, in no place of `Roles`, is bound by no such rule: `introduce` with Carol = Bob is a way, `remove` of herself is none (vGLP #23, Code_inbox.md, 2026-11-06 12:00 UTC, 1).
   - `Pre`, the act's precondition at the proposer's role: `req(P, As)` for each atom the schema requires or deletes there, a cleared atom requiring nothing (12), and `forb(P, As)` for each it forbids there, `As` each argument's place, `at(K)`, a group's, `grp(J, K)`, the places of its leader and its name, or `any`, a variable no place names, as a variable local to the role (vGLP 8a3ae79, Section 5.4); an atom of a reflexive predicate naming that role itself is left out, holding at every person of that person.  Then what the schemas prove (Section 6.2: "where every schema that adds an atom naming x at a role requires or adds at x's role an atom that no schema deletes, the viewer holding the first shows that x holds the second, and an act that forbids the second at x's role is not offered with x"): `unless(P, As)`, `As` `at(K)` at one argument and `any` elsewhere, the act not offered with the person at `K` where the viewer's state holds a matching atom; and `proved(Q, Ws, P, As)`, printed for the place `K` of a `person` field, the `at(K)` of `As`, offering only a person `x` for whom, for each atom `Q(Ws)` the act requires at `x`'s role that a rule proves, `Ws` its arguments `x` and `v`, the viewer, the viewer holds an atom matching some entry of it (Section 6.2: "and, where that person's role requires an atom that the schemas prove of a person from the viewer's records, as above, only a person so proved").  A way the viewer's state satisfies the act is a binding of the places of the `req` entries, distinct `Roles` holding distinct persons, under which the state holds every `req` and lacks every `forb` the binding determines (Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "every binding of the variables of the atoms the schema requires there that a schema-binding makes, distinct roles bound to distinct persons, under which the state holds those atoms and lacks the atoms the schema forbids there that the binding determines"), ways that differ only at `any` being one.
   - `Binds`, the places of the request those `req` entries bind, each `at(K)` and each `grp(J, K)`'s `J` and `K`, beside `M`, in their order: the values a way binds.  No field names one of them (1).
   - `Fields`, the act's fields, `field(I, S, T)`, its place `I`, its label `S`, the name in angle brackets, and its type `T`, in the schema's order (Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A field is for a variable of the schema that those atoms do not bind ... the fields of a line name these variables, of the acts its button names together, in the schemas' order"; "A field is typed, a person by key or picked among the persons the viewer knows"): `person`, a role, picked among the persons the viewer knows, other than the viewer and the persons in the act's other roles but for two possibly one person (Section 6.2: "A field that takes a person in a role offers the persons the viewer knows, other than the viewer and the persons in the act's other roles"); `key`, the peer of an acquaintance schema (8), the key of a person the viewer does not know (Section 6.2: "The field of an acquaintance takes the key of a person the viewer does not know"); `agent`, an agent variable, a person in no role, as GDO-L's label `l` or GC's `<issuer wanted>`, by key or picked among the persons the viewer knows, known or not, the viewer and the persons in the act's roles among them, since Section 6.2's two sentences are of a role and of an acquaintance, and a person in no role is no participant the proposer's machine must know; `text`, a speech-act variable, a text or a new name; and `number`, a numeric variable.  Every place of the request is `M`, in `Binds` or a field's.
   - `Same`, `same(K, J)` for two places of `Roles` possibly one person (GSC 316d875, Section 2), which one person may fill.
   - `Text`, the question of an act with one guarding role, text of its request form, shown with the button (Section 5.4: "A question line naming the proposer is text of the request form, shown with the button"), each word `say(S)` or `at(I)`, the person at place `I`; `[]` for every other act.

   GKK's `establish(p?)`, `[Establish]` with no field and no question, is `button("Establish", [act(establish, 1, [1], [forb(identity, [])], [], [], [], [])])`; GSG's `befriend(p?, q?)`, `Friendship: Alice [Befriend]`, Bob bound by `knows(Bob)` at Alice, is `button("Befriend", [act(befriend, 1, [1, 2], [req(identity, []), req(knows, [at(2)]), forb(friend, [at(2)]), ...], [2], [], [], [])])`.  The term is the `W` of `named(W, D)`, whole, as the type `Widget` of the root `self.glp` holds it, the `Constant` of its text (vGLP e22e2ad, Section 6.3: "the widget W its declaration gives, a term of the construct family"; `DeclaredWidget.drawnAs`).  The Dart side, the canonical compilation's `DeclaredWidget` (`glp_runtime/lib/vglp/canonical.dart`) and the construct family's drawing of the request widget, reads this grammar (GLP #12, Code_inbox.md, 2026-11-06 07:06 UTC, after vGLP-widget).

7. **An approval question's two answers.**  The pragmas give none, so the approval type's declaration carries no label, and the construct family draws the two constants, `yes` and `no`, two of the platform's four words.

9. **A request of the person held.**  A request is taken where the agent guards and is ready for the part it names (Section 5.4: "the Request of {[t]} to itself for each class [t] of a guarded part with no opener named N that it guards and is ready for"). 11. **Constraints.**  A schema's constraint is read in GSC's term form (`programs/gsc/self.glp`): `true`, `and(C1, C2)`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge` and `gt` of two expressions, `succ(X, Y)` and `or(C1, C2)`; an expression a numeric argument, `plus(X, Y)`, `minus(X, Y)` or `mul(X, Y)` (GSC, Definition "Schema with values").  The values are whole numbers, `Integer`.  An equality gives its one numeric variable that a binding leaves free the whole number at which its two sides are equal, where its coefficient is not 0 and there is one; a conjunction what either conjunct gives, taken again with each value given; an inequality gives none (vGLP, Section 5.4: "or the constraint gives the variable finitely many values once those are bound").  A constraint may also hold the signing conditions `signed(U, Y)` and `unsigned(U, Y)`, `U` a role or an agent variable and `Y` a speech-act variable (vGLP dd37d2a, Section 5.4: "signed(u, y) and unsigned(u, y), that the person bound to u signed the speech act bound to y or did not --- which is evaluated when the schema is bound"; GSC 10fcc92, def:numbers): the printed text evaluates them at the binding, `signed` holding where the person the binding gives `U` signed the speech act it gives `Y` or a speech act inside it (`con_holds`, `signing`, `signed_by`); each gives no variable a value, and names no numeric variable, so is local to every role.

12. **Cleared atoms.**  A cleared atom is read in GSC's term form (`programs/gsc/self.glp`, GSC 327e035): an atom among a role's deleted atoms with the wildcard `any` among its arguments, or a name term `ext_any(N)`, `N.*`, among them, as GSN's `unfriend` deletes `listed(role(2), any)` and `member(nterm(ext_any(own(2))))` at its first role (vGLP f8127b1, Section 5.4, "Act schemas as local specifications": "A schema may mark an atom it deletes as cleared, with the wildcard * among its arguments or as the speech act of a name term: the act deletes every atom of the state that it matches, and requires none").  It requires nothing: the atoms a role requires are the atoms it keeps and the atoms it deletes less its cleared ones (`uncleared`, here and in the printed text's `rs_req`), so no condition, no notice `unable`, no widget precondition `req` and no acquaintance test reads one, and the type `Atom` of the view has no alternative from one.  Under a binding the wildcard is itself, `any`, and `N.*` the community name of `N` followed by it, `dot_any(C)` (GSC, Definition "Binding": beta(*) := *), in the printed text's `Val` and `CName`; a class's deleted atoms carry these images, so a schema-binding found by matching them (`mates_of`) has the same cleared atoms, a wildcard matching only a wildcard.  The effect at an agent deletes the deleted atoms with no wildcard and then every atom of the state that the image of a cleared atom matches, whatever its multiplicity, each wildcard standing for any argument, a speech act in a name term (`apply_part`, `clear_split`, `clear_out`; vGLP, ibid.: "yielding d_p less the deleted atoms, less every atom of d_p that a cleared atom matches, plus the added ones").

13. **Reading roles.**  A role that is not guarding and adds and deletes nothing is a reading role, admitted where a guarding role requires an atom naming it, and otherwise a fault of volition, `changes_nothing`, in a verdict `not_grassroots` (GSC 0380a8a; `programs/gsc` from GLP e2b5a802 on), which the compiler refuses as it refuses `volition`.
