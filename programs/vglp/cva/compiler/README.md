# The compiler from a contract to its canonical CVA platform

vGLP's compiler, in GLP: `compile(Name, Contract, Verdict, Intro, Pragmas)` prints the canonical CVA platform of an act-schema contract in vGLP (vGLP, Section 5.4, "Act schemas", "Act schemas as local specifications" and "The contract's words"), between the lines `%% compile begin` and `%% compile end`, or the lines saying why it does not.  `compile/4`, with no pragmas, and `compile/3`, with no introduction predicate either, remain.  The modules: `cvac.glp`, what is printed from the contract; `text.glp`, the text every platform shares; `local.glp`, whether the contract is a local and effective specification; `self.glp`, the types; `pragmas.sh`, the reader of a contract's pragmas.  The scripts that run it are `programs/vglp/cva/compile.sh`, `programs/apps/compile.sh`, `programs/cssn/compile.sh` (CSSN's) and `cascade.sh`.

## The pragmas

vGLP's paper says what the pragmas carry and where the compiler puts them (Section 5.4, "The contract's words"; Section 6.2, "The words a platform shows"), and GSC's paper says how a contract writes them (Section 2, its first paragraph).  The papers name these things and not their term forms; the forms below are the compiler's, for vGLP to put in its paper.

1. **How a pragma line becomes a term the compiler reads.**  `pragmas.sh <NAME.txt>` reads the lines after the line `Pragmas` of the contract's `NAME.txt`, and of the `NAME.txt` of every contract its `% Extends:` line names, each contract extended first, and prints one term, `pragmas(Lists, Acts)`, or `no_pragmas` where the contract's own `NAME.txt` has no line `Pragmas`.  The first line after `Pragmas` is a list: `Contacts: the persons Alice knows.` is `list("Contacts", [the, persons, person, alice, knows, know])`, the words of what it lists in lower case, each also without a final s.  Every other line gives an act for each of its buttons: `Meeting: [Meet] Alice wishes that you know each other.` is `act(meet, "Meet", [], said([person(1), text(" wishes that you know each other.")]))`, the schema the button names, the button in brackets, the fields in angle brackets in order, and the sentence after them, or `none`; the title before the colon must be a clause's of the same `NAME.txt`.  A string is carried as its GLP source text inside a quoted name, `'"Meet"'`, since the compiler's printer writes a constant as it is and GLP has no string concatenation.

2. **Which schema a pragma's act is.**  A line has a button for each act of its clause a person proposes, and each act is the schema whose name is its button's text in lower case, each space an underscore: "a button for each act of it a person proposes, in brackets, each bearing its act's name" (GSC 2991379, Section 2, its first paragraph), `[Establish]` the schema `establish` and `[Post own]` the schema `post_own`; the line's fields and its sentence are each of its acts'.  A button that names no schema of the contract is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.  A schema the request type proposes that no button names is said, in a line of the platform, `%% no button of the contract's pragmas names the request of the schema(S)`, and given no label, so that its request is drawn by its name; no schema of GKK, GSG or GSN is so.

3. **Which predicate a list lists.**  The one predicate of the contract among the words of its pragma: `the persons Alice knows` lists `knows`, `the friends of Alice` `friend`, `the groups Alice is a member of` `member`.  A list naming none, or more than one, is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.

4. **The lists in the view.**  The view the agent writes carries the lists: `view(S, K, Ts)`, `Ts` the lists, each `tab(Name, P)`, its name and the predicate whose atoms in the machine state `S` it lists, `Tab ::= tab(String, Constant)`.  The list holds the predicate and not the atoms, so that no list is under another, as Definition "Volitional Procedure, Interactive Type" requires of the output.

5. **How a sentence's names map to the schema's roles and variables.**  Alice, Bob and Carol are, in that order, the persons of the schema's head: its roles that are not listed roles, then its agent variables, as GSC writes a schema, `befriend(Alice?, Bob?)`, `introduce(Alice?, Bob?; c)`, Carol its agent variable `c` (GSC, Sections 3.2 and 3.3).  The platform writes the sentence into the approval question, `approval(Attempt, Ws, YesNo?)`, `Ws` a list of words, `Word ::= text(String) ; person(Peer)`, each person the one the attempt's schema-binding puts in that place (`sentence_of/3`, printed per schema).  A sentence is put to the agent of each guarding role whose person it does not name: GSG's `Alice asks to be your friend.` is put to Bob, and where the person asked is the one in Alice's place, the question carries no sentence, `[]`.

6. **How a label sits in a widget term.**  The compiler prints two widget declarations into every platform, `Approval =::= approval_sentence.` and `Menu =::= social_menu.`, so that no app writes a `self.vglp`; with pragmas, the request type's carries the labels: `Menu =::= social_menu([label(establish, "Join"), label(message, "Send"), label(message, 3, "text"), ...])`, `label(F, S)` the request whose name is a schema-binding of `F`, labelled with the button `S`, and `label(F, I, S)` its `I`-th place, a field the person fills, named `S`; the `I`-th field in angle brackets names the `I`-th of the request's places that hold a speech act or a number.  The canonical compilation (`glp_runtime/lib/vglp`) writes each label beside its constructor in the widget term: `label(S, W)` around the widget `W` of the labelled constructor, and `label(S, C)` for a constant `C` in a row of buttons; a field named by the declaration is entered `field(S, W)`, its name in place of the name of its declared type, which Definition "Default Widget" makes a field's label; a constructor with no label is written as before, and the construct family draws the name of its type (vGLP, Section 6.2: "A construct family draws the label it is given, and the name of the type where it is given none").

7. **An approval question's two answers.**  The pragmas give none, so the approval type's declaration carries no label, and the construct family draws the two constants, `yes` and `no`.

A contract with no pragmas, GC, GB, GSNP, GAI, CSSN and GFWC today, is compiled as before, but for the two widget declarations.
