# The compiler from a contract to its canonical CVA platform

vGLP's compiler, in GLP: `compile(Name, Contract, Verdict, Intro, Pragmas)` prints the canonical CVA platform of an act-schema contract in vGLP (vGLP, Section 5.4, "The compilation", "Act schemas", "Act schemas as local specifications" and "The contract's words"), between the lines `%% compile begin` and `%% compile end`, or the lines saying why it does not.  `compile/4`, with no pragmas, and `compile/3`, with no introduction predicate either, remain.  The modules: `cvac.glp`, what is printed from the contract; `text.glp`, the text every platform shares; `local.glp`, whether the contract is a local and effective specification; `self.glp`, the types; `pragmas.sh`, the reader of a contract's pragmas.  The scripts that run it are `programs/vglp/cva/compile.sh`, `programs/apps/compile.sh`, `programs/cssn/compile.sh` (CSSN's) and `cascade.sh`.

## The forms the papers leave open

vGLP's paper says what the platform does and what the pragmas carry (Section 5.4, "The compilation" and "The contract's words"; Section 6.2, "The words a platform shows"), and GSC's paper says how a contract writes its pragmas (Section 2, its first paragraph).  The papers name these things and not all their term forms; the forms below are the compiler's, for vGLP to put in its paper.

1. **How a pragma line becomes a term the compiler reads.**  `pragmas.sh <NAME.txt>` reads the lines after the line `Pragmas` of the contract's `NAME.txt`, and of the `NAME.txt` of every contract its `% Extends:` line names, each contract extended first, an owner named in the possessive, `GSC's`, not a contract, and prints one term, `pragmas(Lists, Acts)`, or `no_pragmas` where the contract's own `NAME.txt` has no line `Pragmas`.  The first line after `Pragmas` is a list: `Contacts: the persons Alice knows.` is `list("Contacts", [the, persons, person, alice, knows, know])`, the words of what it lists in lower case, each also without a final s.  Every other line gives an act for each of its buttons: `Meeting: [Meet] Alice wishes that you know each other.` is `act(meet, "Meet", [], said([[person(1), text(" wishes that you know each other.")]]), "Contacts")`: the schema the button names, the button in brackets, the fields in angle brackets in order, the questions after them, and the name of the tab of its contract, the first line of the pragmas of the `NAME.txt` the line is in; the title before the colon must be a clause's of the same `NAME.txt`.  A string is carried as its GLP source text inside a quoted name, `'"Meet"'`, since the compiler's printer writes a constant as it is and GLP has no string concatenation.

2. **Which schema a pragma's act is.**  A line has a button for each act of its clause a person proposes, and each act is the schema whose name is its button's text in lower case, each space an underscore (GSC, Section 2: "a button for each act of it a person proposes, in brackets, each bearing its act's name"): `[Establish]` the schema `establish`, `[Post own]` the schema `post_own`; the line's fields and its questions are each of its acts'.  Where the contract has no schema of that name, the button names the schemas bearing its name with a suffix after an underscore, as GB's `[Swap]` names `swap_cc`, `swap_cb`, `swap_bc` and `swap_bb`, and its line gives an act for each; their fields are the union of their variables in head order, each variable merged in after the one before it in its own schema, the line's fields naming the union's first variables in order, and each schema's act carries the names of its own variables, in its order, an inapplicable field left empty and none after its last named variable (GSC's ruling, Code_inbox.md, 2026-10-24 08:06 UTC).  `pragmas.sh <NAME.txt> <contract term>` reads the schemas and their variables from the term, which the scripts pass it.  A button that names no schema of the contract, by either reading, is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.  A guarded schema, one the request type proposes, that no button names is a fault, and the contract is not compiled, the platform's line saying which, `%% a guarded schema has no button in the contract's pragmas:(S)` (vGLP 932c710, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A guarded schema is proposed by a person, by its button, so the compiler requires a button for every guarded schema; an unguarded one is proposed by its opener's machine and has none").

3. **Which predicate a list lists.**  The predicate named by its name, an underscore read as a space, the longest such phrase of the line, a word also read without a final s (vGLP 110caf4, Section 5.4, "The contract's words"): `the persons Alice knows` lists `knows`, `the friends of Alice` `friend`, `the groups Alice is a member of` `member`, `those Alice is principal of` `principal_of`.  A list naming none, or more than one of that length, is a fault, and the contract is not compiled.

4. **The view.**  The view the agent writes is `view(S, K, A)`: the machine state, the persons the agent knows, and the approvals its person made that are pending, `Approved ::= Proposal`, each by its request name, a request's own and an answer's that of its attempt's part, oldest first, so that no attempt and no list is in it (vGLP e286276, Section 5.4, "The compilation"); a contract with no guarded part has no approvals, its `Approved` being `Constant`; with pragmas, `view(S, K, A, Ts)`, `Ts` the lists, each `tab(Name, P)`, its name and the predicate whose atoms in `S` it lists, `Tab ::= tab(String, Constant)`.  The list holds the predicate and not the atoms, so that no list is under another, as Definition "Volitional Procedure, Interactive Type" requires of the output.  A view is written when the agent starts and whenever the machine state, the persons known or the approvals pending change.

5. **How a question's names map to the schema's persons.**  A name is a word of a capital and lower-case letters that a clause of the contract, or of a contract it extends, writes within a sentence and not first in it: Alice, Bob, Carol, Dave, Alf.  The names a line's clause uses, in the order they first occur in it, are the schema's persons in order: its roles that are not listed roles, then its agent variables, as GSC writes a schema, `befriend(Alice?, Bob?)`, `introduce(Alice?, Bob?; c)`, Carol its agent variable `c` (vGLP, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "A question names the roles of its schema by the names its clause uses, in the order of the roles").  A question naming a name its clause does not use is refused by `pragmas.sh`.  A line's questions are its sentences after its fields: one question is put to each guarding role other than the proposer's, the first guarding role (vGLP 932c710, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "a question into the approval question that Start raises at each guarding role other than the proposer's"; vGLP #20, Code_inbox.md, 2026-10-24 09:21 UTC: "the principal is asked the same question as the agent"); several are put one to each of those roles, in their order (GSC, Section 2: "for each role whose will the act needs beyond the proposer's, in their order, the question the person in it is asked"); more questions than such roles is a fault.  The platform writes the question into the approval question, `approval(Attempt, List(Word), YesNo?)`, `Word ::= text(String) ; person(Peer)`, each person the one the attempt's schema-binding puts in that place (`sentence_of/3`, printed per schema, its question of each role `q(R, Words)`, `ask_of/3` giving that of the role the agent is in).  The question of an act with one guarding role is put to no other person and is text of its request form (6).

6. **What the widget declaration of the request type carries.**  The compiler prints two widget declarations into every platform with a guarded part, `Approval =::= approval_sentence.` and `Menu =::= social_menu(...)`, and the first alone into one with none, which has no request question.  With pragmas, the request type's carries, for each schema the request type proposes:
   - `label(F, S)`, the request whose name is a schema-binding of `F`, labelled with the button `S`;
   - `label(F, I, S)`, its `I`-th place, a field the person fills, named `S`: the fields in angle brackets name, in order, the peer of a meeting schema, a person the act names by key, and then the variables of the schema's head after its roles (vGLP e286276, Section 5.4, "The contract's words": "in the order of the schema's variables after its roles, a person the act names by key among them"), as GC's `swap(p?, q?; u, k, v, k')` has `<issuer given> <amount given> <issuer wanted> <amount wanted>`, GSN's `post(p?, a; x, n)` its one field `<text>`, `x`, GKK's `introduce(p?, q?; c)` its field `<person>`, `c`, and GKK's `meet(p?, q?)` its field `<person>`, the peer `q`; a field is drawn by its name, and none by the name of its type;
   - `act(F, T, M, Ps, Gs, Is)`, one for each guarding role, its place `M` (vGLP 110caf4, Section 6.2: "the button of an act is drawn at each of its guarding roles, beside each row that fills its variable"), where its button is drawn (vGLP 932c710, Section 6.2: "The button of an act that names, beside the person proposing it, a person, a group or an item is drawn beside each one the view lists, on whichever tab lists it, the row filling the act's variable, and the button of an act that names none on the tab of its contract"): `T` the tab of its contract; `M` the place of the person proposing it, its first guarding role; `Gs` the groups it names, `group(J, K)`, for each name term `<u>.y` of its atoms, `J` the place of the role `u` and `K` that of the speech-act variable `y`, which no field names; `Ps` the places of the persons it names beside the proposer that no field names and that lead no group it names; and `Is` the items it names, `item(K, P)`, for each atom of a predicate `P` a list of the app lists, kept or deleted at the proposer's role, with a speech-act variable, a person of another role or an agent variable at place `K` among its arguments, so that a row of that list fills it, as GSNP's `forward(p?, q ; x)`, requiring `item(x)` at `p`, has `item(3, item)`, and a field naming that variable labels it;
   - `meeting(F, I)`, `F` a meeting schema, its peer at place `I`, which a field for the key fills where the peer is not listed (Section 6.2: "Meet with a peer not listed takes a field for the key");
   - `text(F, Ws)`, the question of an act with one guarding role, text of its request form, each word `say(S)` or `at(I)`, the person at place `I`.

   The canonical compilation (`glp_runtime/lib/vglp`, `canonical.dart`, `DeclaredWidget`, `ActPlace`; `constructs.dart`, `_labelled`) writes each label beside its constructor in the widget term: `label(S, W)` around the widget `W` of the labelled constructor, and `label(S, C)` for a constant `C` in a row of buttons; a field named by the declaration is entered `field(S, W)`; and the places of a request around its labelled widget, `act(place(T, M, Ps, Gs, Is, Ks, Ws), W)`, the root's type `Widget` (programs/self.glp) carrying `act(ActPlace, Widget)`.  A constructor with no label is written as before, and the construct family draws the name of its type (Section 6.2: "A construct family draws the label it is given, and the name of the type where it is given none"); a construct family that does not place buttons draws `W`.

7. **An approval question's two answers.**  The pragmas give none, so the approval type's declaration carries no label, and the construct family draws the two constants, `yes` and `no`, two of the platform's four words.

8. **Meeting.**  A meeting schema is one of two party roles, both guarding, whose atoms required or deleted at each role name neither the other role nor a name term built on it (vGLP, Section 5.4, "The compilation": "for a contract the parts of a meeting schema, one with two roles, both guarding, that requires at neither role an atom naming the other, as GKK's meet").  `meeting_peer(N, Me, P)`, printed per meeting schema, gives the peer of a request name `N` beside the agent `Me`, `peer(Q)` or `nopeer`.  A hello carries the request name, `hello(P, N)`; the approval question it asks is `approval(hello(Q, N), Ws, YesNo?)`, `Asking ::= Attempt ; hello(Peer, Proposal)`, asked by `greet`, the person's yes their request `propose(N)`, read by a trigger of its own, `greeted(N)`, as a request is read, its hello sent back, and no next request question asked, the yes answering no request question.  In vGLP's own contracts (`../contracts.glp`), whose befriend requires nothing of the other role, befriend is a meeting schema, and their plays come to know each other by it.

9. **A request of the person held.**  A request is taken where the agent guards and is ready for the part it names (Section 5.4: "the Request of {[t]} to itself for each class [t] of a guarded part with no opener named N that it guards and is ready for").  A request read while the agent is not free for a class of its Request is held, the agent state's last field, and put up again, `again(N)`, after every dispatch that changes the app state or consumes a start, after which alone whether the agent is free can change; the held requests are put up before the held starts, so that a request held while the agent was busy is an approval again before a start of its class is taken, and its person is not asked on that start (vGLP #20, item 1: "so no second question is asked"); it is dropped where the agent is free and no Request is enabled, with the notice `refused(N, p, unable)` where the agent is ready for no part named `N` that it guards or opens (Section 5.4: "with a notice refused(N, p, unable)"); every notice names the request name of the refused attempt's part, `refused(N, q, d)` (vGLP e286276, Section 5.4); `Reason ::= declined ; unable ; unable(List(Atom), List(Atom))`.

10. **The day.**  A contract requires the day where a schema requires `today(d)` at a role.  Its agent takes a fifth argument, the calendar, `Stream(Integer)?`, merged with its triggers as `day(D)`; on each date it replaces `today(d)` in its state and puts up again the starts and the requests it holds.  The agent holds `today(d)` as an atom of the machine state of its app state, which no schema adds or deletes; `atom_of` does not show it, the type `Atom` has no alternative for it, and `show_state` does not compare it, so the view never shows it (Section 5.4, "Act schemas as local specifications": "holds today(d), d the last date read, beside its app state").

11. **Constraints.**  A schema's constraint is read in GSC's term form (`programs/gsc/self.glp`): `true`, `and(C1, C2)`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge` and `gt` of two expressions, `succ(X, Y)` and `or(C1, C2)`; an expression a numeric argument, `plus(X, Y)`, `minus(X, Y)` or `mul(X, Y)` (GSC, Definition "Schema with values").  The values are whole numbers, `Integer`.  An equality gives its one numeric variable that a binding leaves free the whole number at which its two sides are equal, where its coefficient is not 0 and there is one; a conjunction what either conjunct gives, taken again with each value given; an inequality gives none (vGLP, Section 5.4: "or the constraint gives the variable finitely many values once those are bound").

A contract with no pragmas, as vGLP's own (`../contracts.glp`), is compiled as before, but for the two widget declarations.
