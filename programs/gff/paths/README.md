## The model, in short

- **Graph and deciders:** unchanged from the old study (same functions, same draws from `random.Random(s)`), so for the same q and s they are the same grid, friendships, authors and deciders.
- **Friend pointers** (GDO-L's preamble): every friend of an author follows the author at path length 1 without deciding.
- **First following** (GDO-L:1, `follow_friend`): a decider adds a pointer to a friend who has a pointer for that feed.  The contract leaves open which friend: `random`, `first` in the friend list, or `shortest` path to the author.  `shortest` is an oracle, since no agent's state holds path lengths; it is kept as a baseline and reproduces the old first following exactly.  A decider with no such friend waits, and is retried when a friend comes to have a pointer.  There are no relays: following on another's behalf is no act of the contracts.
- **Shortening** (GFF-PS:1, `shorten`): Alice, pointing to Bob who points to Carol, repoints to Carol if Alice and Carol know each other.  Friends know each other; otherwise Bob introduces each to the other (GKK), and each introduction is taken with probability p.  A refused introduction is not asked again.  It runs to a fixpoint.
- **No switching:** the version of 2026-11-09 also had switching by arrival order (a follower trying a second friend and keeping whichever pointer delivers an item first).  It is no act of any contract and was removed on 2026-11-12, from `paths.py`, `async_sim.py`, `sweep.py` and the viewer; git holds it, last at `5fc93257`.  The acts that improve paths are now the contracts' own, one each: GFF-PS's shortening, GFF-FR's redundant following and GFF-FR-BC's back-channel (the last two in `async_sim.py`).
- **Baselines:** the shortest-path tree over the followers' subgraph, which equals the old "after re-asking" result, and the graph distance.

## The measures

Per bin of the author's number of deciders: waiting fraction, path length and its ratio to graph distance, holders per decided follower, the author's fan-in (persons pointing to the author directly), and after shortening the shortenings, introductions taken and refused per follower.

## The asynchronous setting: async_sim.py

    cd programs/gff/paths && /usr/bin/python3 async_sim.py     # writes tables/async.json, about 3 minutes

`paths.py` runs the acts in one tidy schedule (the first following to its end, then shortening to a fixpoint).  `async_sim.py` runs them as a real network would: every act at its own moment in continuous time, each act atomic.  Deciders decide at random times over the first 200 time units; the author posts an item every 10; a delivery hop takes 1 + jitter × U(0,1).  A copy is taken only if its pointer still exists and the item is not yet held, so the first copy is the one recorded (`received`).  Besides following and delivery, each run has the act of one contract that improves the paths (rewritten 2026-11-12, switching removed):

- **GFF-PS** (`shorten_rate` > 0): each follower who is not the author's friend is examined at that rate per item period, and repoints from Bob to the one Bob points to at that moment, introductions taken with probability p.  Each follower has one pointer and moves only to its grandparent, so the pointers stay a tree: no cycles.
- **GFF-FR** (`redundancy` = d > 1): every follower keeps up to d pointers, added by `redundant_follow`, which no one wills.
- **GFF-FR-BC** (also `back_channel`): a follower who got an item first from one pointer forwards it to the others it points to.

No contract combines redundancy with shortening, so d > 1 turns shortening off.  Crash faults (`down_frac`): every machine but the author's is down that share of the time, in stretches of 5 periods on average; it takes and sends nothing while down, and catches up when back.  A copy is sent along every pointer whatever the receiver holds (the sender can't know), and a duplicate is discarded on arrival; catch-up after a new pointer or a recovery sends only what is missing.  Measured at each item: hops along all pointers, followers cut off from the author (none should be), each item's time from posting to arrival and the share on time (within 5 periods), fan-in, load and the counters.  The docstring states the model in full.  The experiment: small world (q = 1), uniform (r = 0, q = 1) and 6-regular graphs, n = 100, density 0.5, seeds 1-3, 3 authors each, 80 items: one pointer (GFF-I) at jitter 0, 1 and 4; GFF-PS at p = 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 1; and one pointer, GFF-PS (p = 0.25), GFF-FR and GFF-FR-BC (d = 2, 3) at 0, 5 and 10% down: 23 settings.

Removing switching left the runs that never used it draw for draw the same: checked 2026-11-12, one pointer, GFF-FR and GFF-FR-BC, with and without faults, give identical samples before and after.  GFF-PS alone never cut anyone off (n = 60, small world and 6-regular, p 0-1, 0 and 10% down).

Findings, 2026-11-12 (`tables/async.json`; means over the three seeds and authors; hops along all pointers at item 80; on time: within 5 periods of posting, and copies per follower per item, over items 30-70; small world / uniform / 6-regular).  No setting cut anyone off.  *Without faults*, one pointer ends at 23.3 / 19.7 / 20.4 hops (best tree through the followers 9.9 / 8.2 / 7.7).  GFF-PS's shortening barely moves that at p = 0 (0-0.02 shortenings per follower: it needs Alice's friend Bob to point to a friend of hers) or p = 0.25 (22.6 / 20.5 / 19.2), cuts it by about a quarter at p = 0.5 (16.7 / 15.9 / 14.2, the author's fan-in 12-17), and at p = 1 puts every follower on the author (1 hop, fan-in about 4,900).  GFF-FR shortens the best path a follower has: d = 2 gives 18.3 / 15.1 / 13.9 hops, d = 3 12.1 / 10.1 / 9.3, near the best tree, at 1.9 and 2.6 copies per follower per item; the back-channel changes hops little but cuts delivery time (regular graph, time from posting to arrival: one pointer 30.7, GFF-FR d = 2 20.7, GFF-FR-BC d = 2 14.5, GFF-FR d = 3 13.7, GFF-FR-BC d = 3 11.6).  *With 10% of machines down*, on time / copies: one pointer 20-29% / 1.04; GFF-PS (p = 0.25) 24-33% / 1.06, no better, since it keeps one pointer; GFF-FR d = 2 53-76% / 2.0; GFF-FR-BC d = 2 91-93% / 2.8; GFF-FR d = 3 92-94% / 2.8; GFF-FR-BC d = 3 93-94% / 4.0.  The back-channel makes d = 2 as reliable as d = 3 at the same cost, as the version with switching found.  Jitter mainly costs delivery time (one pointer on time 99-100% at jitter 0, 35-50% at jitter 4); the hop counts differ with it too, since the time a waiting decider wakes decides whom it follows.

Checked against the paper (Overleaf, 2026-11-09: §3 delivery and correct runs, §4 GFF-PS, §5 GFF-FR and GFF-FR-BC, Appendix C's proofs).  The paper's correct runs are live: a class of transactions that stays enabled is eventually taken.  Back-forwarding was first taken only on the item's first arrival, so a forward to a machine that was down was lost; it is now taken again whenever it becomes enabled (either machine coming back up, Alice adding a pointer), as the Back-channel proposition requires, and the results above are of that version.  On the end states of runs 1,200 time units long, with and without 10% down, Proposition Delivery (whoever points to someone holding an item comes to hold it) and Proposition Back-channel (whoever Alice points to comes to hold what Alice received through another pointer) hold for every item posted by time 900: about 0.4-1.1 million and 0.3 million obligations a run, none unmet once copies in flight land.  The paper's §4 and §5 measurements are of the contracts of 2026-10-28 and marked to be rerun; `paths.py` and `async_sim.py` model the current ones.

## A prototype uniting GFF-PS with GFF-FR-BC: combo_sim.py

    cd programs/gff/paths && /usr/bin/python3 combo_sim.py && /usr/bin/python3 combo_table.py

`combo_sim.py` (2026-11-14) is a copy of `async_sim.py` with GFF-PS:1's shortening allowed beside GFF-FR's redundancy and GFF-FR-BC's back-channel, for a contract uniting them that does not exist yet.  It writes `tables/combo.json` (about 9 minutes; gitignored like the other results) and `combo_table.py` prints the table of means.  Settings without its two flags run exactly as `async_sim.py`'s, draw for draw: checked 2026-11-14, its one-pointer and GFF-PS rows reproduce the findings above to the last digit.  `combine` reads GFF-PS:1 literally over several pointers (Alice has a Dave-pointer to Bob and none to Carol, Bob has one to Carol; one such pair at random); `guard` adds to its body that Alice received an item from Bob by delivery and Bob received that item from Carol by delivery.  Same experiment as above (three graphs, n = 100, density 0.5, seeds 1-3, 3 authors), at 0 and 10% of machines down.

Findings, 2026-11-14 (small world / uniform / 6-regular, no faults unless stated).  GFF-FR-BC at d = 2 with shortening at p = 0.5 ends at 11.9 / 10.5 / 10.5 hops, against 17.6 / 14.6 / 13.7 for GFF-FR-BC alone and 12.2 / 10.0 / 9.3 for GFF-FR-BC at d = 3; time from posting to arrival 11.3 / 10.3 / 10.0 against 18.4 / 15.2 / 14.5 and 15.0 / 12.3 / 11.6; on time with 10% down 94-95% against 91-93% and 93-94%; at 2.66 copies per follower per item against 2.56 and 3.61; the author's fan-in 21-27 against 6.  At p = 0.25 it gains almost nothing (17.3 / 14.2 / 13.5), and at p = 1 it is GFF-PS's star.  Unguarded, it cuts followers off: with several pointers, Bob's redundant pointer may lead to someone who follows through Alice, and a group whose pointers all stay inside it has no pointer path to the author, which neither contract alone ever does.  At p = 0.5, 0.27-0.76% of followers are cut off at the end (5-14% at p = 1).  They still receive every item, since a neighbour outside the loop back-forwards into it; without the back-channel (GFF-FR with shortening) they do not, and the small world reaches 98% of followers with no faults.  The guard keeps the gain (12.1 / 10.8 / 9.6 hops at d = 2, p = 0.5) with about an eighth of the shortenings (0.66 per follower against 5.6) and cuts the loops by one to two orders of magnitude, but not to none: 0-0.01% cut off at the end with no faults, 0-0.12% with 10% down.  A plausible reason, not checked: each guarded shortening is safe within one item's tree of first arrivals, but followers shorten on different items, whose trees differ.  At d = 3 the guarded union ends at 5.2 / 4.4 / 4.0 hops with no one cut off, but the author's fan-in rises to 107-206.

## The graph, the authors and the seeds

They are those of `paths.py`: `timed.py` draws them with its functions from `random.Random(s)` in the same order, so for the same q and s the grid, the friendships, the authors and their followers are the same.  From the same generator it then draws the order of arrivals, the posting offsets, and one integer from which are seeded the faults, one generator per fault rate, and each scenario's own choices, one generator per scenario.  Every scenario of a run therefore has the same arrivals, the same posting times and, at a fault rate, the same faults.

## Time

The steps are discrete.  The friendships, those of the grid and the long-range ones, and the following requests, a decided follower coming to want to follow their author (GFF:2), are put in one random order, interleaved, and one arrives at each step.  Each author posts an item every K = 5000 steps, at an offset of their own drawn uniformly in 0 to K - 1; with 80,000 to 150,000 arrivals, an author posts about 20 to 30 items while the graph grows.  A message --- an item, an update, a request to follow through a friend or to cease --- sent at one step is received at the next: a message takes one step per hand.  A friendship takes effect between the two at once; what each machine does on it is done by that machine.  The run ends 2K steps after the last arrival, and the items posted up to K steps after the last arrival are measured, each having had at least K steps to arrive.

## GFF

1. Friends follow each other (GFF:2), and the author sends every item they post to every friend (GFF:5).
2. Updating (GFF:6 and its Algorithms line): Carol updates every friend whenever Carol comes to follow an agent, by friendship or through a friend, and a new friend upon befriending, with the list of all Carol follows.  An update is a message.  Alice's machine learns from the updates which friends follow whom, in the order the updates arrive.
3. Delivery (GFF:4): the author sends every item to every friend; Carol forwards to Alice, who follows Bob through Carol, every item of Bob's feed not signed by Alice.  An item is never sent to one who signed it.
4. Following via Friend (GFF:3 and its Algorithms line): when Alice wants to follow Bob, Alice's machine follows Bob through the first friend known to follow Bob, and through no other while that following stands.  With no friend known to follow, Alice waits, and follows when an update tells her of one.  Alice follows through Carol by a message to Carol, after which Carol forwards Bob's items to Alice (GFF:5).  Alice comes to follow Bob, and updates her friends, when she sends it.
5. A follower who becomes a friend of the author ceases every following of the author through a friend and follows by friendship.  The mail and the contract leave it open; GFF:2 confines wanting to follow through a friend to one who is not a friend.
6. No one ceases to want to follow, so Unfollowing (GFF:4) is not exercised, and no following rests on a friend who has ceased to follow.
7. An item carries the signatures of its path, the author's first (GKK:4 on GFF-chooser).  The hands an item took to reach a follower are the signatures on the first copy the follower received.

## GFF-PS

GFF-PS:1 and its Algorithms line, on arrival: "Alice, who received via Carol an item of Bob's feed signed by Alice's friend Dave, follows Bob through Dave instead of via Carol", by a message to each of the two.  Carol is one Alice follows Bob through, the item having come by that following; an item that came from a friend Alice does not follow through, by redundant forwarding, switches nothing.  With several such friends, Alice takes the one nearest the author on the item's path; the contract leaves which one open.  Under GFF alone Alice receives Bob's items only through Carol, so every signer is nearer Bob than Carol.

## GFF-FR

GFF-FR:1 and its Algorithms line: Alice's machine follows Bob through two friends known to follow Bob when there are two, and through the one when there is one, adding a second when another becomes known.  Two variants of the choice, as the mail asks:

1. random: the two chosen uniformly at random among the friends known to follow;
2. far apart: the two whose paths to Bob share the fewest agents, the author not counted, and with one already followed through, the one whose path shares the fewest with its path.  A path is the chain of first followings from the friend to Bob at the time.  This is the simulator's knowledge: the contract gives Alice no means to learn a friend's path before items arrive, and the mail does not say how she learns it.

GFF-FR:2 and its Algorithms line, on arrival: Alice, who follows Bob through Carol and Dave and receives an item first from Carol, forwards it to Dave, unless Dave signed it.

## Comparison 3, GFF-PS and GFF-FR together

Both rules at once, with GFF-FR's random and far-apart choices.  GFF-PS:1 replaces the following through which the item arrived.  If Alice already follows Bob through Dave, the following via Carol ends and Alice is left with one; GFF-FR's Algorithms line then adds a second known friend, other than Carol.  The contracts leave open whether Carol may be taken again; if she may, Alice takes Carol again and leaves her again on every item that comes via Carol signed by Dave.  Before GFF's revision this comparison was not run: an item forwarded redundantly by Eve, who follows Bob through Alice, came to Alice signed by Eve, and GFF-PS:1 had Alice follow through Eve, a cycle; Delivery now sends no item to one who signed it, and Shortening names the following it replaces.

## Faults

Each agent, independently, stays up for a stretch drawn as ceil(Exp(mean 1/F)) steps and then goes down for ceil(Exp(mean D)) steps, F being the fault rate per agent per step and D the mean down stretch, here D = 1000 and F = 0, 0.00001 and 0.0001, about 0%, 1% and 9% of agent-steps down.  A down agent takes nothing: the messages sent to it, and the arrivals concerning it, are kept and taken, in the order sent, when it comes back.  A down author does not post.  Faults are applied to every scenario; comparison 2 is GFF against GFF-FR at the fault rates above 0, and comparison 1 is GFF against GFF-PS at 0, the tables giving every scenario at every rate.

## The measures

They are per item, by the author's number of decided followers in powers of ten as in `paths.py`, pooled over the seeds.  An item's followers are the decided followers who want to follow the author and follow them, by friendship or through a friend, when it is posted; the waiting fraction is of those who want to follow at posting and do not.  Over an item's followers:

1. the share reached by the end of the run;
2. the hands the first copy took to reach each;
3. the steps it took, which the mail does not ask for and which show the faults, hands being unchanged by a delay;
4. the messages of the item sent, to friends and forwarders as well as to the followers, over the followers reached, the items reaching none left out.

For GFF and GFF-PS without faults: the hands against the distance in the friendship graph as it stands at posting, which is the tree of shortest paths over the whole graph, and against the distance in the subgraph of the author and all following the author at posting, which is the tree over the followers; the same by the fifth of the arrivals in which the item was posted; and the switches.  The tables also give, per scenario, the update messages and the messages to follow through or cease to follow through a friend.

## Sweeping the parameters: sweep.py

    cd programs/gff/paths && /usr/bin/python3 sweep.py

Runs `paths.py`'s own functions over the graph, n, decider density, strategy and p, and writes every author's results to `tables/sweep.json`: 1,065 runs (1,215 with the switching experiment, which took most of the 18 minutes on a 10-core Mac).  Graphs, in three families with matched mean numbers of friends (4, 6, 8, 12): *small world* (Kleinberg, r = 2, q = 0, 1, 2, 4), *uniform* (Kleinberg, r = 0, q = 1, 2, 4) and *regular* (d = 4, 6, 8, 12).  Experiments: *graphs* (each graph's expansion and distance), *reach* (every graph, density 0.02-0.9, exact k), *shorten* (6 and 12 friends per family, density 0.3/0.6, p 0-1), *size* (6 friends per family, n 30-200), and *natural* (`paths.run` itself).  The *switch* experiment of 2026-11-09 (switching by arrival order) was removed on 2026-11-12 with switching itself; git holds it, last at `5fc93257`.  The docstring says what each measures.  The small-world runs draw exactly as the first sweep did; that sweep's results (it also had q = 8 and density 0.9) are kept as `tables/sweep-v1.json`.

Findings, 2026-11-08.  First sweep (small world only): reach is site percolation from the author's friends, with a threshold in decider density that falls with q (0.62, 0.29, 0.20, 0.13 and 0.08 for q = 0, 1, 2, 4 and 8); below it only deciders within two friendships of the author follow; first-come paths are 4-6 times the graph distance and grow with n; shortening has a cliff between p = 0.5 and 0.75, past which the author's fan-in jumps.  Second sweep (expanders): the regular and uniform graphs stay expanders as n grows (spectral gap about 0.26 and 0.19, while the small world's falls), but paths after the first following are only 7-11% shorter than on the small world with as many friends, and on all three graphs they grow about as the 0.36-0.41th power of the number of people while the graph distance grows logarithmically.  Reach thresholds on the regular graph sit near 1/(d-1); expansion lowers the threshold most at 4 friends (0.40 against the grid's 0.62).  Shortening's cliff and the small-author limit are the same on every graph.
