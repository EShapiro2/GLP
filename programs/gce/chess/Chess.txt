Chess
Chess extends Friendship.
The *rules of chess* are the Laws of Chess of FIDE, the International Chess Federation.
Alice and Bob are persons.
1. ■ Start: Alice and Bob may jointly start a new game of chess, provided they are friends and neither has started a game of that name; Alice then plays white and Bob black, from the first position.
2. ■ Move: Alice may move in a game against Bob, provided it is her turn and the move is legal by the rules of chess; the game is then at the position after the move.
3. ■ Win: Alice may claim a game against Bob, provided Bob is to move and is checkmated; Alice has then won the game.
4. ■ Resign: Alice may resign a game against Bob; Bob has then won the game.
5. ■ Draw: Alice and Bob may jointly agree a draw in a game.
6. ○ Fair play. Alice will choose each of her moves herself, with no help from a chess engine.
Logic pragmas
Chess extends Friendship.
The *rules of chess* are the Laws of Chess of FIDE, the International Chess Federation.
Alice and Bob are persons, Game a game, Move a move, Board and Next positions, Side a number.
1. ■ Start: Alice and Bob may jointly start Game at Board, provided Alice and Bob are friends and Board is the first position.
2. ■ Move: Alice may make Move in Game against Bob from Board to Next, provided Alice and Bob play Game at Board, Alice has the color Side in Game, Side is the side to move of Board, 1 is the legality of Move on Board and Next is the position after Move on Board.
3. ■ Win: Alice may claim Game against Bob at Board, provided Alice and Bob play Game at Board, Bob has the color Side in Game, Side is the side to move of Board and 1 is the mate of Board.
4. ■ Resign: Alice may resign Game against Bob at Board, provided Alice and Bob play Game at Board.
5. ■ Draw: Alice and Bob may jointly draw Game at Board, provided Alice and Bob play Game at Board.
6. ○ Fair play. Alice will choose each of her moves herself, with no help from a chess engine.
Logic pragmas.
Vocabulary: x plays g against y at b = at(x,g,y,b); x has the color s in g = color(x,g,s); x has started g = started(x,g); x has won g against y = won(x,g,y); x has drawn g with y = drawn(x,g,y); x has made m in g at b = made(x,g,m,b); b is the first position = first(b); v is the legality of m on b = legal(v,m,b); c is the position after m on b = after(c,m,b); s is the side to move of b = side(s,b); v is the mate of b = mate(v,b).
Functions: b is the first position; v is the legality of m on b; c is the position after m on b; s is the side to move of b; v is the mate of b.
x and y play g at b: x plays g against y at b and y plays g against x at b.
After x and y start g at b, which requires x has not started g and y has not started g: x has started g, y has started g, x and y play g at b, x has the color 1 in g and y has the color 2 in g.
After x makes m in g against y from b to c: no longer x and y play g at b, x and y play g at c and x has made m in g at b.
After x claims g against y at b: no longer x and y play g at b and x has won g against y.
After x resigns g against y at b: no longer x and y play g at b and y has won g against x.
After x and y draw g at b: no longer x and y play g at b, x has drawn g with y and y has drawn g with x.
