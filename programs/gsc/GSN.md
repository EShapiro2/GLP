A *child* is a natural person not of age.  A *computational agent*, or *AI agent*, is a program that holds a key pair of its own and takes acts of the contract with it.  An *agent* is a person, a child or a computational (AI) agent.  Note that under the above, a *non-person agent* can only be a child or an AI agent.  A child also takes the pronoun 'they', and an AI agent 'it'; Alice and Bob are variables naming distinct agents.

1. *Message.*  An agent may send a message to a friend, with content they created or received from another agent.
2. *Group.*  An agent may create a group, and is then its leader and first member.
3. *Joining.*  The leader of a group may add a friend to it, by an act of both.
4. *Leaving.*  A member of a group may leave it by their own act; when the leader leaves, the group ends.  Ending the friendship of a member and the leader ends the membership.
5. *Group message.*  A member of a group may send a message to the group, with content they created or received from another agent, and the leader delivers it to every member.

## The schemas, and what they realise

The schemas are in `GSN.glp`, in GSC's notation above each term: GSG's `establish`, `befriend` and `unfriend`, and nine more over seven predicates more; every role but establish's requires `identity`, and the lists below leave it out.  A group is named by its leader `a` and a name `n`, a speech-act variable; content `x` is a speech-act variable.  `member(a, n)` in the state of `q` says that `q` is a member of the group `n` led by `a`; `listed(q, n)` in the state of the leader `a` says that `q` is a member of `a`'s group `n`; `closed(n)` at `a` says that `a`'s group `n` has ended.  The groups follow those of CSSN's contract (`programs/cssn/CSSN.glp`), without children and without items.

- Clause 1: `message(p?, q ; x)` --- at `q`, `friend(p)`, +`received(x, p)`; `p` guards it, and their state does not change.
- Clause 2: `create(a? ; n)` --- at `a`, not `member(a, n)`, not `closed(n)`, +`member(a, n)`, +`listed(a, n)`.
- Clause 3: `join(a?, q? ; n)` --- at `a`, `member(a, n)`, `friend(q)`, +`listed(q, n)`; at `q`, `friend(a)`, not `member(a, n)`, +`member(a, n)`.
- Clause 4: `leave(a, q? ; n)` --- at `a`, -`listed(q, n)`; at `q`, -`member(a, n)`; and, the leader leaving, `close(a? ; n)` --- at `a`, +`closed(n)`, -`member(a, n)`, -`listed(a, n)`, after which no member can post to the group and the leader delivers nothing in it; and `drop(a, q ; n)` --- at `a`, not `friend(q)`, -`listed(q, n)`; at `q`, -`member(a, n)` --- which no role guards, so ending the friendship of a member and the leader ends the membership (CSSN #6's request, approved by Udi, 2026-10-19).
- Clause 5: `post_own(a? ; x, n)` --- the leader's own message: at `a`, `member(a, n)`, not `posted(x, n)`, +`posted(x, n)`; `post(p?, a ; x, n)` --- a member's message, sent to the leader: at `p`, `member(a, n)`; at `a`, `member(a, n)`, `listed(p, n)`, `friend(p)`, not `posted(x, n)`, +`posted(x, n)`; and `deliver(a, q ; x, n)` --- the leader delivering it to every member: at `a`, `member(a, n)`, `posted(x, n)`, `listed(q, n)`, `friend(q)`, not `delivered(x, q, n)`, +`delivered(x, q, n)`; at `q`, `member(a, n)`, +`received_in(x, a, n)`.  A post and a delivery require that the member be the leader's friend, so a group's messages travel between friends.
