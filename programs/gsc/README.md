# The syntactically-grassroots checker, and the compiler

Decides whether a contract is **syntactically grassroots**, and when it is not, which condition failed, at which schema, role and atom; and compiles the schemas of a contract that is into their volition-guarded transactions, printed as the LaTeX of the paper's displays, befriend's in Section 5.1 and the swap's in Appendix B.3, and in the general form of `def:compile`, each role's lines over its extent, for a schema with a listed role or a name term.

The specification is the paper --- *Formal Grassroots Social Contracts* (`/Grassroots/GSC`, `main.tex`): Section 3 for the language and its conditions, one definition for a contract of agent roles and listed roles (GSC ac7ad63, which brought listed roles into Section 3 and removed Appendix D); Section 5.1 for the compilation; and the appendix "Values in Speech-Act Schemas" (`app:numbers`, `sections/18-numbers.tex`, at GSC 1934846) for values.  The signed term, reflexive predicates and contracts with wards are GAI's extension of the language, no longer GSC's (GSC 83b951c and 1a2a5f3): GAI's paper, `/Grassroots/GAI`, its appendix "Contracts with Wards", `sections/wards.tex`, carries them, and CSSN's paper, `/Grassroots/CSSN`, `sections/appendix-schemas.tex`, the condition on an agent-named contract with wards, `def:sg-wards`; the checker keeps them, held for GAI.  The federation extension of speech-act schemas --- seated and constituent roles, thresholds, reach conditions, name variables, rootedness, cohesion, circles and `federate` --- left GSC's paper (GSC f3c4f43) and its code left this directory, git holding it, until GFWC's paper carries it (Coordination #7, 2026-10-20).  Speech-act schemas allow values of any type (Section 3.2, the paragraph after the example of `def:schema`; `def:numbers`, `def:binding-numbers`): a value argument, a constraint on a schema's value variables and a multiplicity, a positive whole number, on an atom added or deleted, in the term form "Writing a contract" gives; a value names no agent, so traceable provenance and volition do not concern values and are decided on the contract with its multiplicities and its constraints dropped; `def:unobstructed` asks that an atom be added at a binding of a schema, which satisfies the schema's constraint, and is decided on the contract as written, a value matched by equality and an introduction choosing its own values ("Values").  Nothing of any paper is restated here or in the code: every procedure names the definition it decides, and the definition is read in the paper.

A contract is syntactically grassroots (`def:syntactically-grassroots`) when it has an unobstructed (`def:unobstructed`) introductory act (`def:introduction`), when the set of predicates occurring in it has traceable provenance (`def:grounded`), and when it satisfies volition (`def:volition`), one definition for agent roles and listed roles alike.  Appendix B.4 certifies the currency by hand, and the checker returns the same verdict and the same set of predicates of traceable provenance.  A contract whose introductory act is obstructed may still be certified by GAI's appendix "Contracts with Wards": when it has an introduction with wards (`def:introduction-wards`) --- a nonempty set of marks each with a guardianship (`def:guardianship`), an introductory act unobstructed but for the marks (`def:unobstructed-marks`), and for every pair of marks a relay act with a ward role and a free role and one with two ward roles (`def:relay`), the act and the relay acts forbidding besides the leaving marks of the contract (`def:leaving`) --- and has traceable provenance and volition, it is grassroots with wards (`thm:grassroots-wards`), and the verdict names the introductory act, the marks, their guardianships, the relay acts the search found and the leaving marks.  The protocol realising it is volitionally grassroots; where the verdict names no leaving mark it is grassroots as well (GAI's appendix, after `thm:grassroots-wards`).

A listed role `pi[rho(*, u_2, ..., u_r)]` (`def:role`) has the agent role `pi` as its base, so it joins `pi` in the role graph (`def:volition`); it is no agent role, so an atom it requires is no psi of `def:grounded`; only an agent role carries the mark `?` (`def:schema`), so a listed role guards no component; and the first part of clause 2 of `def:unobstructed` asks that no listed role add an atom of the predicate of an atom the introductory act forbids.  An introductory act is guarded in both its roles, so they are agent roles, and has no name term (`def:introduction`), and so has each schema of arity one of clause 1 of `def:unobstructed`.  Section 7.1 certifies GSN, the social network of Section 3.3, whose close takes the listed role `Alice[listed(*, n)]`, and the checker returns the same verdict; GSNP, GAI's, carrying the signed term, is certified the same way.  A name term of a schema is `<pi>` for an agent role `pi` of it, or `sigma.y` (`def:role`), so a contract with a name term based on anything else, an agent variable among them, is malformed.  When the first conjunct fails and the other two hold, the wards are tried: a contract with no listed role and no name term by GAI's appendix, and one with them by `def:sg-wards` (CSSN's paper), which asks it to be agent-named (CSSN's `def:agent-named`), its introductory act, relay acts and schemas of arity one with agent roles and no name term, and no listed role adding or deleting a mark in M, a leaving mark, an atom of the predicate the introductory act adds, or an atom of g or of w of the guardianship of a mark in M; such a contract is syntactically grassroots with wards.  CSSN's contract is certified this way.

A cleared atom (GSC 327e035, Definition Speech-act schema) is an atom marked `-` with the wildcard `*` among its arguments, or in place of the speech-act variable of a name term among them, as GSN's unfriend clears `listed(Bob, *)` and `member(<Bob>.*)` at Alice: it deletes every atom of the agent's state that it matches and requires none.  The marks divide `E_i` into `+(i)`, `-(i)`, the atoms marked `-` with no wildcard, `*(i)`, the cleared atoms, `not(i)` and `=(i)`, and a role requires `=(i) u -(i)`; so a cleared atom puts no edge in the role graph (`def:volition`), is no psi of `def:grounded`, and is no change a role makes, `+(i) u -(i)` being nonempty at every role that is not a guarding role and at some role (`def:schema`).  `def:schema` requires that no atom of `+(i)` be obtained from an atom of `*(i)` by replacing its wildcards; clause 1 of `def:unobstructed` asks for a schema of arity one that "requires and clears nothing"; and `def:compile` assigns `c'_p := ((c_p \ -) \ K_p) u +`, `K_p` holding every atom of `c_p`, with its multiplicity, that matches a cleared atom of a role whose extent holds `p`.  A name term `<pi>.*` of a cleared atom has the base `pi`, as CSSN's paper reads it ("in a cleared atom, `<u>.*`, its base the agent role u"), so a schema with one has a name term and is printed in the general form; it is no name term `sigma.y`, and the compiled form asks no freshness of it.  A binding may send two roles to one person only where neither adds or deletes an atom naming the other (`def:binding`); a cleared atom naming the other, which deletes every atom it matches, each naming the other, counts there as deleting one.

The compiler is Section 5.1.  A schema of agent roles, written `schema` and with no name term, compiles to the assignment, the proviso and the guard of `def:compile`, printed in the short form of the displays of Section 5.1 and Appendix B.3, whatever the contract it is in, as Section 5.1 prints befriend of GSN.  Any other schema is printed in the general form, a line per agent, as `def:compile`'s display has it and Section 5.1 prints close's (Udi, 2026-10-23, to GSC #7, reported by GSC #7 Cowork).  The compilation is defined for contracts the checker certifies, so the compiler runs the checker first and compiles nothing for a contract that is malformed or that is not syntactically grassroots.

## Running it

Needs the Dart SDK and this repository; nothing else.  A contract is its owner's and lives with the owner's code, one copy, in the term form of "Writing a contract" below: GSC's are `programs/gsc/GKK.glp`, the keys and knowledge every contract extends, `GSG.glp`, the social graph, and `GSN.glp`, the social network; GAI's `programs/gai/GSNP.glp`, the social network with provenance; the currency's is `programs/currencies/coins/GC.glp`, the bonds' `programs/currencies/bonds/GB.glp`, with numbers, the child-safe social network's `programs/cssn/CSSN.glp`, AI agents' `programs/gai/GAI.glp` and the federation's `programs/federation/GFWC.glp`.  A REPL session on the file prints the contract's term, and a session on this directory decides it.  From the repository root:

    cd glp_runtime
    printf '/path/to/GLP/programs/gai/GSNP.glp\ncontract_named(gsnp, C).\n:quit\n' | bin/glpc
    printf '/path/to/GLP/programs/gsc/\n:limit 1000000\ncheck(<the term the first printed>, V).\n:quit\n' | bin/glpc

or interactively, entering the directory path at the prompt to load the program and then a goal:

    GLP> /path/to/GLP/programs/gsc/
    ✓ Loaded program: /path/to/GLP/programs/gsc/
    GLP> check([schema(befriend, [], [rs(guarded, [atom(friend, [role(2)])], [], [], [atom(friend, [role(2)])]), rs(guarded, [atom(friend, [role(1)])], [], [], [atom(friend, [role(1)])])]), schema(unfriend, [], ...)], V).
    V = syntactically_grassroots

The REPL stops a goal at 10000 reductions unless told otherwise, and a contract of twenty-nine schemas takes more: enter `:limit 1000000` before the goal, as the test script does.

The tests are `bash programs/gsc/test_gsc.sh` from the repository root; it reads each contract from its owner's file and passes the term in.

## The compiler

    cd glp_runtime
    printf '/path/to/GLP/programs/gsc/\n:limit 1000000\ncompile_contract(gsnp, <GSNP's term>).\n:quit\n' | bin/glpc

prints the sixteen displays of GSNP, and `compile_schema_in(gc, <GC's term>, swap).` prints one.  GLP has no string concatenation, so a display is printed one token to a line.  A newline is whitespace to LaTeX, so those lines are the display and are pasted into a paper as they stand; `test_gsc.sh` compares the displays the paper writes, befriend's in Section 5.1 and the swap's in Appendix B.3, with what the compiler prints, the whitespace removed.  The closing comma and full stop of the paper's displays belong to the sentences around them rather than to the compiled form, and are not printed.

A display of the short form is laid out as Udi ruled (GSC #3 to Code, 2026-10-18 21:49 UTC): the assignments of the roles on one line; the required atoms on one proviso line and the forbidden atoms on the next, each in role order, a line omitted when it has no atom; and the guard on a line of its own.  A guarding role that adds and deletes nothing, which `def:schema` admits, is assigned `c'_{\Alice} := c_{\Alice}`, as `forward` of GSNP is at Alice.  A role's required atoms are written with `\in`, a single one as Appendix B.3 writes the swap's and several distinct ones listed, as Section 5.1 writes befriend's, `\mathit{identity}, \mathit{knows}(\Bob) \in c_{\Alice}` (GSC 21c1b28); a multiset of required atoms with an atom twice in it is written as `def:compile` writes it, `\{...\} \subseteq c_{\Alice}`, and so are several in the general form.  The paper's displays leave out two lines of the compiled form of `def:compile` and the compiler leaves them out too: that what is added and what is deleted at an agent are disjoint, and that the state of an agent in a role that is not guarding changes, which holds under the other provisos at such a role, `def:schema` requiring it to add or delete an atom.  The compiler names role i by its index, role 1 `\Alice`, role 2 `\Bob`, role 3 `\mathit{Carol}` and role 4 `\mathit{Dave}`, and not by the names a paper's table gives them.

A display of the general form has a line per agent, as Section 5.1 prints close's: first the agent of each agent role, in role order, `c'_{\Alice} := ...`, its line joining what every role whose extent holds it adds and deletes there; then the other agents of the listed roles' extents, `c'_q := ... \qquad (q \in \mathrm{ext}_c(\Alice[\mathit{listed}(\ast,n)]),\ q \neq \Alice)`, their line joining what those roles do.  The agent of an agent role is in the extent of a listed role `pi[rho(*, u_2, ..., u_r)]` where `pi` requires `rho(that role, u_2, ..., u_r)`, as close's leader requires `listed(\Alice, n)`; it is not where `pi` forbids that atom; and where the schema says neither, as of the parent in CSSN's `child_close`, the agent's line is written for each case, the case after it in parentheses, `(\Bob \in ...)` and `(\Bob \notin ...)`.  Then the required atoms of every role on one proviso line, an agent role's included in `c_{\Alice}` and a listed role's in `c_q`, the extent after the line in parentheses, `\qquad (q \in \mathrm{ext}_c(...))`; then the forbidden atoms the same way; for every name term `sigma . y` of the schema that occurs in no atom required at a role, that it is an argument of no atom of the configuration (GSC 6d4f147); the constraint; and the guard, the guarding roles of the schema, which are agent roles, written as Section 5.1 writes a guard, `\text{guarded by } \{\Alice\}` (GSC f3c4f43).  A schema of more than one listed role has a proviso line per listed role after the agent roles' line, and a line for `q` per set of the listed roles whose extents hold it.  The line on what is added and what is deleted at an agent is left out, as the displays of Section 5.1 leave it out.  A listed role is printed as `def:role` writes close's, `\Alice[\mathit{listed}(\ast,n)]`, its agent role, then its predicate with `\ast` in the first place and its arguments after.  The atoms of a set are written apart by a comma, as in the short form.

A role that clears an atom has `K_S` in its assignment, as `def:compile` writes it, `c'_{\Alice} := (c_{\Alice} \setminus \{\mathit{friend}(\Bob)\}) \setminus K_{\Alice}` for GSN's unfriend, an empty half left out as before, and a line after the assignment saying which atoms `K_S` holds, in `def:compile`'s words, `\text{where } K_{\Alice} \text{ holds every atom of } c_{\Alice} \text{, with its multiplicity, that matches } \mathit{listed}(\Bob,\ast) \text{ or } \mathit{member}(\langle\Bob\rangle\cdot\ast)`, with the side condition of its assignment, in the short form a line per role that clears, after the assignments, and in the general form after each line of an agent.  The wildcard is written `\ast` and `<Bob>.*` as `\langle\Bob\rangle\cdot\ast`, as Section 3.3 writes unfriend.  The proviso of `def:compile` that what is added at an agent is disjoint from what is deleted there and from `K_p` is left out, as the displays of Section 5.1 leave out that line; no display of the paper yet shows a schema that clears.

`def:compile` applies to a schema with values as written (the appendix "Values in Speech-Act Schemas"), whose example prints GB's redemption of a matured bond in the form below, the constraint on a proviso line of its own.  A value argument is written as its variable or its number; a multiplicity k on an atom A as `k\cdot A`, as GB's paper writes it (GB, Appendix A), so that `\{k\cdot A\}` is the multiset with A k times; a role's required atoms carrying a multiplicity are written as `def:compile` writes a multiset, `\{...\} \subseteq c_{\Alice}`; and the constraint is a proviso line of its own after the forbidden atoms and before the guard, `\text{provided } d \le t`, `gt(X, Y)` as `X > Y`, `le(X, Y)` as `X \le Y`, `succ(X, Y)` as `Y = X + 1` and `or(C1, C2)` as `C1 \lor C2`, as GB's paper writes its constraints.  A schema with no number is printed as before.

A schema's name is no part of its compiled form, so `compile_named` puts it in an `\iffalse ... \fi` before each display, which TeX skips.  A contract the checker does not certify is not compiled, and the message names the reason; for a malformed one it names each fault, its schema and role, in the paper's words.

Printing reaches the person, so the module's certificate is refused on load (`[CERTIFICATE REFUSED] gsc ... calls send_to_user/1`) and it carries no signature.  It loads and runs as before.

## The notation

    cd glp_runtime
    printf '/path/to/GLP/programs/gsc/\n:limit 10000000\nnotation_contract(<the term>).\n:quit\n' | bin/glpc

prints the schemas of a contract from its term, in the notation of Definition Speech-act schema, `def:schema` of Section 3.2 and `def:numbers`: `tau(pi_1, ..., pi_k; x_1, ..., x_m) :` on a line, a guarding role carrying `?`, the semicolon and the variables left out where the schema has none, and then its marked multisets `E_1 ... E_k`, each on a line of its own, `at pi_i:`, labelled by its role as Section 3.3's table labels its columns "at Alice" and "at Bob".  Within `E_i` the unmarked atoms come first, then those marked `not`, `+` and `-`, the order of the paper's displays.  The text is plain, as befriend of Section 3.2 is written above: `not` for the paper's negation sign, `<Alice>.n` for the name term `<Alice> . n` and `Alice[listed(*, n)]` for a listed role (`def:role`), `*` for the wildcard of a cleared atom, `-listed(Bob, *)` and `-member(<Bob>.*)`, among the atoms marked `-`, `sgn(x, Alice)` for a signed term (GAI's Definition Speech-act schema), and a contract declaring predicates reflexive opens with them, `Reflexive predicates: friend` (GAI's appendix "Contracts with Wards", "Reflexive predicates").  Roles are named as the compiler names them, Alice, Bob, Carol, Dave and `p_i` beyond the fourth; a variable is written as it is named.  A schema with values is printed as `def:numbers` writes it, as GB's paper does (GB, Appendix A): a value argument as its variable or its number, the value variables in the head with the other variables in the order of the head, the constraint after the head and a bar, `mint_bond(Alice?; d, k, t) | d > t :`, with `>`, `<=`, `t1 = t + 1` and `or`, and a multiplicity k on an atom A as `k.A`, a full stop for GB's centred dot as for that of a name term, `+k.bond(Alice, d)`.  The text is printed a token to a line between the lines `%% notation begin` and `%% notation end`, an empty line ending a line of the text; `cascade.sh` joins the tokens into each record's `schemas.txt`.

## The entry points

| Goal | What it returns |
|---|---|
| `check(C, V)` | the verdict on the contract `C`, given as a term, with or without numbers |
| `intro_acts(C, A)` | its introductory acts (`def:introduction`), in the order of the contract, each `intro_act(Schema, unobstructed)` where some introduction of it meets `def:unobstructed`, and `intro_act(Schema, obstructed(Faults))` otherwise, the faults of its first introduction, as the verdict names them for the first introductory act: GKK's is `[intro_act(acquaint, unobstructed)]`, and GSG's names befriend too, obstructed by the `knows` of the other it requires, which no schema of arity one adds (GSC, Section 3.6, the example after `def:introduction`) |
| `traceable_preds(C, E)` | its predicates of traceable provenance, the largest set having it by `def:grounded`, each psi required at an agent role |
| `compile_contract(Name, C)` | prints the compiled form of every schema of it, refusing it under `Name` |
| `compile_schema_in(Name, C, Schema)` | prints the compiled form of one schema of it |
| `notation_contract(C)` | prints its schemas in the notation of Definition Speech-act schema (`def:schema`, `def:numbers`), as "The notation" above says |
| `check_named(Name, V)` | the verdict on a fixture of `contracts.glp` that stands alone |
| `traceable_of(Name, E)`, `contract_named(Name, C)`, `compile_named(Name)`, `compile_schema(Name, Schema)` | the same of such a fixture |
| `check_variant(Name, C, V)`, `traceable_variant(Name, C, E)`, `intro_acts_variant(Name, C, A)`, `variant(Name, C, D)`, `compile_variant(Name, C)`, `compile_schema_variant(Name, C, Schema)` | the same of a fixture that changes the contract `C`, as its owner writes it, in one place, the schemas the fixture adds or changes written with their roles' names, `schema(Name, Names, Vars, Roles)` |
| `wards(C, W)` | its introduction with wards (GAI's appendix "Contracts with Wards"), searched for whatever the verdict: `introduction_with_wards(Act, Marks, Guardianships, Relays, Leaving)` or `no_introduction_with_wards`; none for a contract with a name term not based on an agent role, which `def:role` does not admit |
| `guardianships(C, M, G)` | every guardianship of every mark of the set `M`, for `M` |
| `leaving(C, M, L)` | for each choice of a guardianship for each mark of the set `M`, in the order the search tries them, `leaving(Gs, Ls)`: `Gs` the guardianships chosen and `Ls` the leaving marks of `C` for `M` with them (`def:leaving`) |
| `wards_of(Name, W)`, `wards_variant(Name, C, W)`, `leaving_of(Name, M, L)` | the same of a fixture, and of a variant |
| `relay_h(C, H)` | for the introduction with wards `wards` finds, every atom a relay act of it requires at a ward role that clause 2 of `def:relay` admits as an atom of a predicate h and as nothing else (GSC 410a440), each `h(Schema, Role, H)`, in the order of the relay acts and of their roles; `[]` where there is no introduction with wards |

### The verdicts

A verdict is `syntactically_grassroots`, by `def:syntactically-grassroots`, one definition for agent roles and listed roles alike (GSC ac7ad63); `grassroots_with_wards(Act, Marks, Guardianships, Relays, Leaving)`, for a contract with no listed role and no name term, by GAI's appendix "Contracts with Wards"; `syntactically_grassroots_with_wards(Act, Marks, Guardianships, Relays, Leaving)`, for one with them, by `def:sg-wards` (CSSN's paper); `not_grassroots(Faults)`; or `malformed(Faults)`.  Each fault of `not_grassroots` is one of

    no_introductory_act
    obstructed(Schema, Role, Atom, unobtainable)
    obstructed(Schema, Role, Atom, blocked_by(Schema, Role, Atom))
    obstructed(Schema, Role, Atom, reflexive)
    obstructed(Schema, Role, Atom, listed_role(Schema, Role, Atom))
    obstructed_jointly(Schema)
    untraceable(Predicates)
    volition(Schema, Role, Role)

in the order of the conjuncts of `def:syntactically-grassroots`.  `unobtainable` is clause 1 of `def:unobstructed`, which skips a required atom of a reflexive predicate naming the role (GAI's `def:unobstructed`), `blocked_by` is clause 2, naming the schema, role and added atom that obstruct, and `reflexive` is clause 3 of GAI's `def:unobstructed`, the atom being one of a reflexive predicate naming the role, which every state holds.  `listed_role` is the first part of clause 2 of `def:unobstructed`, naming the schema, the listed role and the atom it adds of the predicate of an atom the introductory act forbids.  Where the act has values, a fault is a condition that no choice of them meets ("Values"); `obstructed_jointly` is an act each condition of which some choice of its values meets, and no choice all of them.  Clause 1 of `def:unobstructed` asks for a schema of arity one, guarded in its role, so an agent role, with no name term, requiring nothing, and forbidding nothing or only the atom required, the introductory act then requiring that atom once at the role (GSC b2bb8be); the second part of its clause 2 is asked of the atoms agent roles add; and an introductory act has agent roles and no name term.  `untraceable` names the predicates of the contract outside the largest set having traceable provenance, the set `traceable_of` returns; the verdict is over the whole set and the faults both name what is missing and, through `traceable_of`, what has it.  A `volition` fault is raised only when the role graph is disconnected and some connected component of it holds no guarding role, a guarding role being an agent role, and names two roles the role graph does not join, the first role and the first one it does not reach, so at arity two it names the pair that has no edge.

When the introductory act fails and the other conditions hold, the wards are tried (GAI's appendix "Contracts with Wards"), and a contract with an introduction with wards is `grassroots_with_wards(Act, Marks, Guardianships, Relays, Leaving)`, or, agent-named and meeting `def:sg-wards`, `syntactically_grassroots_with_wards(...)` of the same arguments: `Act` the introductory act unobstructed but for the marks, `Marks` the set M, `Guardianships` a `guardianship(M, Iota, G, W)` per mark, the `(m, iota, g, w)` of `def:guardianship`, `Relays` the relay acts found, each `relay(Schema, Party, Party)` with a party `ward(Role, Mark, GuardianRole)` or `free(Role)`: for each mark one with a free role, then for each pair of marks, in the order of the set, one with a ward role of each; and `Leaving` the leaving marks of the contract for M with those guardianships (`def:leaving`), in the order of its marks, `[]` where it has none.  Clause 3 of `def:leaving` names "an atom g(sigma) of the guardianship of a mark in M", the guardianship taken for that mark, as GAI's Lemma "Wards and guardians" reads it; so the leaving marks are taken for each choice of a guardianship for each mark, with it, and the verdict names the guardianships and the leaving marks of one choice.  The search is existential, as `def:introduction-wards` is: the first set of marks, guardianships and act that satisfy it are reported, and nothing is reported of a failed search, whose verdict is `not_grassroots(Faults)` as before; `wards`, `guardianships` and `leaving` are there to look.  A schema deletes an atom at a role if the atom is marked `-` there or matches a cleared atom there (GAI 11d7133, before `def:guardianship`), so the clauses of `def:guardianship`, `def:leaving` and `def:relay` that speak of deleting read `-(i)` and `*(i)` both: no schema clears an atom of g or of w (clause 1 of `def:guardianship`, GAI a81e827); a cleared `v(*)` asks for a cleared atom of w matching `w(*)`, so no schema clears an atom of v, by clause 5 as GAI 11d7133 writes it, GAI a81e827's reading of it, with u a role or an agent variable, waiting on GAI; a cleared atom with the wildcard as an argument deletes an atom naming any role; and a mark, having no argument, is deleted by no cleared atom.  Clause 1 of `def:guardianship` (GAI a81e827): the roles at which a schema adds or deletes some mark in M are at most one, and the marks in M it adds or deletes there at most one.  The record of clause 2 of `def:relay` (GAI a81e827) is `h'(rho, args)`: a schema adding m at rho adds it at the role at which it adds `w(rho)`, and a schema adding `v(rho)` and not m at rho requires it at a role requiring `w(rho)` and adds it where it adds `v(rho)`; every predicate of the contract of the right arity is a candidate for h'.  `def:introduction-wards` names no predicate h of clause 2 of `def:relay`, so the verdict names none; `relay_h` gives, for the relay acts found, each atom required at a ward role that clause 2 admits as an atom of h and as nothing else.

No answer is left open: whether some choice of the values the introductory act's introduction chooses meets the clauses is decided exactly ("Values"), and every verdict is one of those above.

A contract with a schema not formed as `def:role` and `def:schema` require is malformed: its head lists its variables, distinct agent and speech-act variables, and its atoms are over its roles, those variables and its signed terms; `+(i)` and `-(i)` are disjoint at every role, and `+(i) u -(i)` is nonempty at every role that is not a guarding role and at some role, a guarding role being one that may add and delete nothing (GSC 70afdc4); only an agent role carries the mark `?`; and every name term is based on an agent role of its schema.  A contract that declares a predicate reflexive and has a schema adding or deleting, at a role, the atom of that predicate naming that role is malformed too (GAI's appendix "Contracts with Wards", "Reflexive predicates"), whatever its roles.  So is a contract with a schema whose signed term violates what GAI's Definition Speech-act schema requires of it, or whose role is not an agent role, and one with a listed role that `def:role` does not admit: of a role that is not an agent role of its schema, or with a signed term among its arguments, a signed term occurring only in an atom marked `+`.  It is refused, its verdict `malformed(Faults)`, each fault a

    head_not_variable(Schema, Entry)
    head_repeated(Schema, Var)
    not_in_head(Schema, Var)
    added_and_deleted(Schema, Role, Atom)
    added_and_cleared(Schema, Role, Atom)
    wildcard(Schema, Role, Atom)
    listed_wildcard(Schema, Role)
    changes_nothing(Schema, Role)
    no_role_changes(Schema)
    reflexive(Schema, Role, Atom)
    signed(Schema, Role, Atom, not_added)
    signed(Schema, Role, Atom, not_required)
    signed(Schema, Role, Atom, not_agent_role)
    listed_base(Schema, Role)
    listed_signed(Schema, Role)
    listed_guarded(Schema, Role)
    multiplicity_not_added(Schema, Role, Atom)
    multiplicity_not_numeric(Schema, Role, Atom)
    multiplicity_not_whole(Schema, Role, Atom)
    multiplicity_wildcard(Schema, Role, Atom)
    constraint_not_numeric(Schema, Arg)
    constraint_not_conjunction(Schema, Or)
    constraint_not_linear(Schema, Product)

naming the schema and, where there is one, the entry, the variable, the role and the atom, and no condition is decided for it.  `head_not_variable` is an entry of the head that is not a variable, `head_repeated` an entry the head lists before, and `not_in_head` a variable occurring in the schema that its head does not list.  `added_and_deleted` is an atom both added and deleted at a role, `added_and_cleared` an atom a role adds that one of its cleared atoms matches, `wildcard` an atom not marked `-` carrying the wildcard and `listed_wildcard` a listed role with the wildcard among its arguments, which are arguments of the schema (`def:role`), `changes_nothing` a role that is not guarding and adds and deletes nothing, a cleared atom being no change, and `no_role_changes` a schema no role of which adds or deletes an atom.  `not_added` is a signed term in an atom not marked `+`, `not_required` one whose speech-act variable occurs in no atom its role requires, each a requirement of GAI's Definition Speech-act schema, which admits a signed term whether or not its role is a guarding role; and `not_agent_role` one whose role is a listed role, GSC having admitted `sgn(y, pi)` for `pi` an agent role until GSC 1a2a5f3.  `name_term_base` is a name term whose base is not an agent role of its schema, `<x>` for an agent variable `x` among them, named by its schema and the name term.  `listed_base` is a listed role whose base is not an agent role of its schema, `listed_signed` one with a signed term among its arguments, and `listed_guarded` one carrying the mark `?`, each named by its schema and its index.  Of the numbers (Section 3.2: "an atom added or deleted may carry a multiplicity", "a constraint on its numeric variables"): `multiplicity_not_added` is an atom carrying a multiplicity that its role requires or forbids and neither adds nor deletes, `multiplicity_not_numeric` an atom whose multiplicity is no value argument, `multiplicity_not_whole` one whose multiplicity is written as a number that is no positive whole number (`def:numbers`), `multiplicity_wildcard` an atom carrying a multiplicity and the wildcard, "an atom marked + or -, with no wildcard" alone carrying one, so a cleared atom none (`def:numbers`, GSC 593e8a7), `constraint_not_numeric` an argument of the constraint that is no value argument, `constraint_not_conjunction` a disjunction in the constraint, and `constraint_not_linear` a product of two expressions each carrying a value variable, the constraint being "a conjunction of linear equalities and inequalities over its value variables and values" (`def:numbers`).  A value variable is a variable of the schema, listed in its head with the others, so one occurring in an atom, a multiplicity or the constraint and not in the head is `not_in_head`, one listed twice `head_repeated`, and a number in the head `head_not_variable`.  An atom with a multiplicity is an atom added or deleted to `def:schema`'s requirements, so `added_and_deleted` and `changes_nothing` are decided on the atoms, the multiplicities dropped.

## Writing a contract

A contract is a GLP term: the list of its speech-act schemas (`def:schema`), or, where it declares predicates of arity one reflexive (GAI's appendix "Contracts with Wards"), `reflexive(Predicates, Schemas)`.  Write it in a file of its own, in its owner's directory, as `programs/gsc/GSN.glp` is written: the type declarations below, then `procedure contract_named(Constant?, Declared).` and a clause returning the contract under its name.  `contracts.glp` holds only the checker's fixtures.

    NameTerm   ::= own(Integer) ; own_var(Constant) ; ext(NameTerm, Constant)
                 ; ext_any(NameTerm).
    Arg        ::= role(Integer) ; pvar(Constant) ; svar(Constant)
                 ; nterm(NameTerm) ; sgn(Constant, Integer) ; num(Constant) ; any.
    Args       ::= [] ; [Arg | Args].
    Atom       ::= atom(Predicate, [Arg, ...]).
    Mult       ::= Atom ; times(Arg, Atom).
    Guard      ::= guarded ; unguarded.
    RoleKind   ::= listed(Integer, Predicate, Args).
    RoleSpec   ::= rs(Guard, Add, Del, Keep, Forb)
                 ; crs(RoleKind, Guard, Add, Del, Keep, Forb).
    Constraint ::= true ; and(Constraint, Constraint)
                 ; eq(Expr, Expr) ; le(Expr, Expr) ; lt(Expr, Expr)
                 ; ge(Expr, Expr) ; gt(Expr, Expr) ; succ(Arg, Arg).
    Expr       ::= Arg ; plus(Expr, Expr) ; minus(Expr, Expr) ; mul(Expr, Expr).
    Schema     ::= schema(Name, Names, Vars, [RoleSpec, ...])
                 ; cschema(Name, Names, Vars, [], [RoleSpec, ...])
                 ; nschema(Name, Names, Vars, Constraint, [RoleSpec, ...]).
    Body       ::= [Schema, ...] ; reflexive([Predicate, ...], [Schema, ...]).
    Contract   ::= Body ; environment([Predicate, ...], Body).

A schema's `Names` is the names of its roles, in order, as its clause names them, `['Bob', 'Alice']` for GSN's post, a listed role named by its base (GSC 25b6b3c, Section 2: "A name in a pragma is the schema's role of that name, the schemas naming their roles as the clauses do"); the notation prints each role by its name, and vGLP's pragma reader maps a pragma's name to its role by them.  A schema written without `Names`, `schema(Name, Vars, Roles)` and the rest, as the owners' contracts not yet rewritten write it, is read as before, its roles printed Alice, Bob, Carol and Dave by their place.  A contract names its environment predicates by `environment(Preds, Body)` (GSC 25b6b3c, Section 3.2 and the appendix "Values in Speech-Act Schemas": atoms over values "which the machine holds from startup and the platform keeps current, and which no schema adds or deletes"), as GB's `today` once Currencies writes it so: the checker takes an introductory act requiring or forbidding an atom of one as obstructed, `obstructed(Name, I, Atom, environment)` ("an unobstructed introductory act requires no environment atom ... and forbids none"), and the notation opens with the line `Environment predicates: ...`.

A schema's `Vars` is its variables, agent and speech-act, each written `pvar(X)` or `svar(Y)`, in the order of its head (GSC 0228e79: Section 3.2, Definition Speech-act schema, `tau(pi_1, ..., pi_k ; x_1, ..., x_m)`); every agent and speech-act variable the schema has, in its atoms, in their name terms and signed terms, and in the arguments of its listed roles, is listed, once, and the checker refuses a schema whose head is otherwise.  A role is named by its index, so `role(1)` is the schema's first role and the roles of `schema(Name, Vars, Rs)` are `role(1)` to `role(K)` for `K` the length of `Rs`; `pvar` is an agent variable and `svar` a speech-act variable; `sgn(Y, I)` is the signed term `sgn(y, pi_I)` of GAI's Definition Speech-act schema, the speech act `y` signed by the agent in role `I`, which an atom a schema adds may carry where role `I` is an agent role and requires an atom with `y` among its arguments, guarded or not.  `Guard` is `guarded` or `unguarded`, recording the mark `?`, which only an agent role may carry; and `Add`, `Del`, `Keep`, `Forb` are the role's `+(i)`, `-(i)`, `=(i)` and `not(i)`.  An agent role is written `rs` and a listed role `crs`, its `Guard` `unguarded`: `listed(I, Rho, Args)` is the listed role `pi_I[rho(*, u_2, ..., u_r)]` of `def:role` (GSC 8064cfb), `I` the index of its agent role `pi_I`, `Rho` the predicate and `Args` the arguments `u_2, ..., u_r`, its extent being the agents `q` with `rho(q, beta(u_2), ..., beta(u_r))` in the state of the agent in role `I` (`def:extent`); `own(I)` is the name term of the schema's `I`-th role, `<pi_I>`, and `ext(Sigma, Y)` the name term `Sigma . y`; `own_var(X)`, `<x>` for an agent variable, is read and refused, `def:role` admitting no such name term.  A cleared atom is written among a role's `Del`, with the wildcard `any` among its arguments or `ext_any(Sigma)`, the name term `Sigma.*`, which Definition Name term (`def:role`, GSC 14c921c) admits in a cleared atom, as GSN.glp writes unfriend's `atom(listed, [role(2), any])` and `atom(member, [nterm(ext_any(own(2)))])`; the checker refuses the wildcard anywhere else.  A schema with a listed role may be written `schema` or `cschema`; the third argument of `cschema` is `[]`, where reach conditions stood until GSC f3c4f43 removed them, as the owners' contracts write it.  One definition decides every contract (GSC ac7ad63); whether a contract has a `crs`, a `cschema` or a name term decides only which paper's wards are tried, GAI's appendix for one with none and CSSN's `def:sg-wards` for one with any.

Speech-act schemas allow values of any type (Section 3.2, the paragraph after the example of `def:schema`): "an argument of an atom may be a value or a value variable, a schema may carry a constraint on its value variables, which every binding satisfies, and an atom added or deleted may carry a multiplicity, a count of copies and so a positive whole number".  The term form is Currencies' GB.glp (b9369200), which this checker reads; it writes a value as a number.  `num(X)` is a value argument: the value variable `X` where `X` is a name, and the number `X` where `X` is a number, so `num(d)` is the variable d and `num(0)` the number 0.  A value variable is listed in the schema's `Vars` with its agent and speech-act variables, in the order of its head.  `times(K, A)`, in `Add` or `Del`, is the atom `A` with the multiplicity `K`, a value variable or a positive whole number, so that `+k.A` adds `A` k times; `Add`, `Del`, `Keep` and `Forb` are lists of atoms and of atoms with multiplicities, and the checker refuses a multiplicity in `Keep` or `Forb`.  A schema with a constraint is `nschema(Name, Vars, Constraint, Roles)`, the constraint "a conjunction of linear equalities and inequalities over its value variables and values" (`def:numbers`): `true`; `and(C1, C2)`; `eq(X, Y)`, `le(X, Y)`, `lt(X, Y)`, `ge(X, Y)` and `gt(X, Y)`, X = Y, X <= Y, X < Y, X >= Y and X > Y; and `succ(X, Y)`, Y = X + 1, as GB.glp writes it; each X and Y an expression, a value argument, `plus(X, Y)`, `minus(X, Y)` or `mul(X, Y)`, a product with one factor carrying no value variable.  The checker refuses `or(C1, C2)`, which GB.glp's term form admitted, and a product of two value variables.  GB's mint of a bond, written as GB's paper writes it (GB, Appendix A),

## Why it terminates

As Section 3.6 says.  Bindings are finite once taken up to renaming: the schemas name no person (`prop:anonymity`), so the pair of people the conditions quantify over may be fixed, and a binding is then an assignment of the schema's agent variables to the two of them.  Unobstructedness matches one atom against those the finitely many schemas add: a match sends a value variable to the value it meets, and whether some values meet the clauses is decided by eliminating the variables of a finite formula one at a time, each elimination giving finitely many cases ("Values"); volition is a connectivity check on the roles, and traceable provenance is a greatest fixpoint over finitely many predicates, reached by dropping and repeating.  An introduction with wards is a search over what the contract supplies, all of it finite: the sets of its marks, its predicates of arity zero; for each mark the guardianships `(m, iota, g, w)` with `iota` a mark and `g` and `w` predicates of arity one of the contract; for each choice of a guardianship per mark, its leaving marks, among its marks; its introductory acts; and its schemas as relay acts, with their roles as the parties.  The conditions of `def:unobstructed-marks`, `def:relay` and `def:sg-wards` are on the text, up to renaming a role, and take no binding but those of the introductory act.

## One thing to know about the reading

Clause 2 of `def:unobstructed` quantifies over every binding of every schema, and matching the forbidden atom constrains only the variables it meets.  A match is a binding of `def:binding`, sending two roles to one person only where neither adds or deletes an atom naming the other.  The checker completes the rest with names nothing else uses, and decides the clause against that completion.  That is the binding that settles it: the clause excuses a binding that sends a role to the other party, and a variable the match left free can be sent elsewhere, so if the completion is not excused then some binding obstructs.

The speech acts an introduction binds its speech-act variables to are taken signed once, an utterance signed by one person, and a signed term matches none of them: `sgn(y, pi)` is `(beta(y), beta(pi))` with `beta(y)` a speech act, a speech act signed at least twice (`def:binding`, Section 3.1).  That is the choice that decides the clauses in the contract's favour, and `def:unobstructed` lets the introduction make it: clause 2 holds against an act adding a signed term whenever the forbidden atom's speech act is signed once, and clause 1 loses nothing, since the act of arity one it asks for requires nothing and so, by `def:schema`, adds no signed term.

A community name is not an agent and not a speech act (Section 3.1), so an atom with a name term among its arguments matches no atom of the introductory act, which has none.

"A value names no agent, so the conditions below do not concern it" (Section 3.2).  A value has no base (`def:grounded` at GSC 1934846), so it makes no record and no edge of a role graph: traceable provenance and volition are decided on the contract with its multiplicities and its constraints dropped, every value argument staying in its atom and naming no one.  `def:unobstructed` asks that an atom be added "at some admissible binding" of a schema, and "a schema may carry a constraint on its value variables, which every binding satisfies" (Section 3.2); so an atom required is supplied when an atom the schema of arity one adds equals it, numbers included, at a binding satisfying that schema's constraint (GSC #5 Cowork, Code_inbox 2026-10-20 22:26 UTC), and an atom forbidden is added by a schema at such a binding.  The checker matches a value by equality, sending a value variable to the value it meets (`nm_supplied`, `nm_other_number`), and decides the schema's constraint on the values the match gives it (`nm_admits`, `nm_excludes`, `nm_forbid_admits`, `nm_forbid_excludes`).  An atom carrying a multiplicity is added at every binding, a multiplicity being a positive whole number (`def:binding-numbers`).

## Values

A binding maps each value variable to a value, under which the schema's constraint holds and every multiplicity is a positive whole number (`def:binding-numbers`); an introduction chooses its own values; and the constraint is "a conjunction of linear equalities and inequalities over its value variables and values", so the conditions "stay decidable, the constraints being linear" (the appendix "Values in Speech-Act Schemas", GSC bcc1891; GSC #7 Cowork, Code_inbox 2026-10-23 23:00 UTC).  The checker decides every case exactly.

A value variable a constraint compares ranges over the numbers, and one used as a multiplicity over the positive whole numbers.  The values are a countable set with a fractional amount among them (the appendix), and a linear constraint with rational coefficients that some numbers satisfy is satisfied by rational ones, so the checker takes the numbers to be the rationals.  A number the contract writes is read exactly: a whole one as it is, and one that is not whole as the binary fraction GLP holds; the arithmetic is GLP's, on whole numbers of 64 bits.

The images of the introductory act's atoms carry its value variables, and a match of another schema's atom against one of them records the equations it rests on (`match_atom`, `self.glp`).  A binding of that schema is then a conjunction of linear atoms over the act's values and the schema's own (`inst_conj`, `unobstructed.glp`): the schema's constraint, each multiplicity at least one and whole, each value variable the match binds equal to its image, and the equations.  An atom the act requires at a role is supplied where some schema of arity one has such a binding (clause 1), with the act requiring it once where that schema forbids it; an atom the act forbids is added where some schema has such a binding that the clause does not excuse (clause 2).  The act is unobstructed at an assignment of its agents where some values satisfy its own constraint, every condition of clause 1 and none of clause 2 at once.

`linear.glp` decides it.  A conjunction has a solution where, its rational variables eliminated by Fourier-Motzkin, which is exact over the rationals, and its whole ones by Cooper's method, which is exact over the integers, what is left is true.  The clause 1 bindings are chosen with the act's values, so their variables join the conjunction.  The clause 2 bindings are those of a universal question inside the existential one --- the act chooses its values so that no binding adds what it forbids --- and are eliminated first, leaving a formula over the act's values, which is negated: their rational variables by Fourier-Motzkin, with the act's values as parameters, and their whole ones by Cooper's method, where the least whole number above a bound with a rational part needs that part's ceiling, so each rational value x of the act is written n + f, n whole and 0 <= f < 1, and the ceiling of a sum over the f's takes one of finitely many values, each a case (`decomp_defs`, `cooper`).  `nm_two_between` has introductions only where `0 < x < y < 1`; `nm_cover` and `nm_gap` forbid `date(x)`, added below 5 and above 5, or above 6; `nm_evens` and `nm_counts` forbid `date(k)`, `k` whole, added at the even whole numbers, or at all; `nm_half` has `2k = 3`, which no whole `k` meets.

The faults of an obstructed act, at its first assignment, are each condition that no choice of its values meets with its constraint, in the order of the roles, and the faults the values do not touch; where every condition is met at some choice and no choice meets them all, the fault is `obstructed_jointly(Act)` (`nm_joint`).  A `blocked_by` fault names the first block that adds the atom at some choice.
