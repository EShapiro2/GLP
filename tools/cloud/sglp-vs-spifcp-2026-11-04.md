# The sGLP simulator against SpiFcp: architecture, design and code

Cloud #3, 2026-11-04.  Udi's question: why the sGLP simulator is slow, 2 ms an answer at 100 agents and 33 ms at 1,000 over a simulated year, the memory growing to 1 GiB.  His standard: no deviation from the FCP design and implementation; every deviation is reviewed and justified, the one approved being a heap in place of a linear queue.  Nothing was run; this is a reading of the two sources.

Read on the SpiFcp side (`EShapiro2/FCP`, the ASPIC release, `ASPIC/Aspic-release/SpiFcp`): `Source/spi_monitor.cp`, the monitor and the scheduler, whole; `Source/spi_utils.cp` and `Source/library_server/spi_library.cp`, the send and receive requests; `Source/spifcp/spc.cp`, the transformation's output phase, where a process's communications become one request; `Merged EMULATOR/spicomm.c`, the C step: `spi_post`, `spi_step`, `transmit_biomolecular`, `transmit_delay`, `get_sum_weight`, `set_nowp`; `Documentation/BNF.txt`, `stochastic.txt`, `weighter.txt`, `object.txt`; `Examples/circadian`.  On the sGLP side: the paper whole (`svGLP-Stochastic-Volitional-GLP` at 20a8913); `programs/sglp/monitor.glp`, `sglp.glp`, `self.glp`, `README.md`; `social_graph/harness.glp`, `population.glp`, `profiles.glp`, `graph.glp`, `self.glp`, `run.sh`; `glp_runtime`'s `when_idle` (`runtime.dart`, `scheduler.dart`), the mutual reference kernels and `_output` (`body_kernels.dart`), the root `self.glp`'s `merge/3`, `mwm/2`, `send_to_user/1`; Cloud #1's `fcp-vs-glp-engine-2026-11-02.md`.

## 1. SpiFcp's design, as the code has it

A channel is a vector of twelve slots: its type, its base rate, its reference count, a send anchor and a receive anchor, each the head of a circular doubly linked list of offers, the send weight and the receive weight (the sums of the offers' multipliers), the weighter, and the links of the circular list of channels, anchored at `BasedAnchor` (`spi_monitor.cp`, `new_channel`, `queue_channel`).  The lists are destructive: `store_vector` writes a slot in place.

A process with a sum of communications is compiled into one FCP clause that writes one request, `start(ProcessId, Operations, Message, Chosen)`, on the scheduler's FCP channel and calls `P.comm`, whose clauses are guarded on `Chosen` (`spc.cp`, `stochastic`).  `Operations` lists every offer of the sum, `{SPI_SEND or SPI_RECEIVE, ChannelName, Channel, Multiplier, Tag}`.  So a waiting process is one suspended FCP process on one variable, whatever the size of its sum.

The monitor's `scheduling` loop reads the scheduler channel.  A `start` is handed to C, `execute(SpiOffset, {SPI_POST, ...})`: `spi_post` walks the offers, links each on its channel's send or receive list at the end, adds its multiplier to the channel's weight, and leaves `Message` and `Chosen` unbound (`spicomm.c`, `spi_post`).  An instantaneous channel is served at once.  Each `start` costs one monitor reduction and one C call.

When the system is idle the Logix kernel binds `Wakeup`, which the monitor asked for with `processor # machine(idle_wait(Wakeup))`.  The monitor's clause on `Wakeup = done` calls C once, `execute(SpiOffset, {SPI_STEP, Now, BasedAnchor, Now', Wakeup'})` (`spi_monitor.cp` line 1039).  `spi_step` walks the circular channel list twice: the first pass sums the weights, base rate times send weight times receive weight for each unblocked channel; the second draws a uniform, walks until the running sum passes it, and transmits on that channel: `transmit_biomolecular` takes the first receive offer and the first send offer whose owners differ (or random ones under `randomize`), unifies the receiver's `Message` with the sender's, binds both `Chosen` to the winning tags, and `discount` removes the losers of each owner's sum from their channels, adjusting the weights; `set_nowp` advances the clock by minus the logarithm of a second uniform over the sum of the weights, Gillespie's direct method (`spicomm.c` lines 1307 to 1456, 1669 to 1837).  The two processes wake by the binding of `Chosen`, each reduces its `.comm` clause once.  The monitor asks for the next idle.  One transmission is therefore one C step, a scan over the channels, and about three FCP reductions.

The clock is the vector `SPITIME`; any process reads it by `read_vector` (`object.txt`), with no message and no monitor reduction.  The record stream gets `start(PName)` per request and `[Now, end(PName(RCId, arrow, CNId)), end(...)]` per transmission, the process names and the channel names, a few words, formatted later by `spi_record`.  The step function has an FCP version too, `sum_weights` and `logix_total`, used when `spicomm` is not linked (`SPIOFFSETS`), so the design is a C kernel with the same algorithm in FCP as the fallback.  `random()` is C's, unseeded.

## 2. sGLP's design, as the code has it

The population runs in one machine.  Each agent is its platform goal, `graph#start`, its person process, `population#person/8`, its screen, and what they spawn; the harness adds the network, `mwm/2` over the agents' output streams and a balanced tree of `deliver/4` goals to their input streams, the monitor, and the log's sink, `send_to_user/1` on a mutual reference (`harness.glp`).

A question: the platform's asking clause writes `ask('Menu', menu_w(X?))` on its ask stream and goes on as `ask1`; `merge/3` goals the compilation adds carry the ask to the person process; the person process's clause draws a seed (`random/4`), spawns the profile's person procedure, `homophile_menu(Y?, H?, K?, Mon?, Scr?)`, and the relay, `relay_1(A, 'Menu', E?, Y, Mon, Log)`, and recurs (`population.glp`).  The person procedure draws `K` (`random/4`), appends `rated(1/week, Tok)` to the monitor's reference (`stream_append`, a kernel) and spawns `homophile_pick(Tok?, K?, S1?, M?)`, which suspends on `Tok` (`profiles.glp`).  The monitor takes the request, draws the delay, `random/4`, `delay/3`, `seconds/2`, two arithmetic goals, and `qmerge`s a node into the skew heap, about 2 plus log2 P reductions, P the pending requests (`monitor.glp`).

A release: with the queue empty the scheduler wakes the monitor through `when_idle` (`runtime.dart`, `wakeIdle`; `scheduler.dart`, `drainWithStatus`); the monitor's third clause pops the root, `qmerge(L?, R?, Q)`, log2 P reductions, sets the clock and binds `Tok` to `go`.  `homophile_pick(go, K, S, menu(Ss, _, same(P?)))` reduces, its guard `ground(Ss?)` walking the list, and calls `one_of(Ss?, S?, P)`, which is `length/2` over the list and `nth/3` to the drawn position, about one and a half reductions per element of the list, n in all.  The relay, waiting on `ground(X1?), ground(X2?)`, two more walks, has handed the copy to the person goal and spawned `answer_1`, which waits on `ground(Y3?)`, then appends `time(Time)` to the monitor's reference and `entry(Time?, A, 'Menu', menu(X1?, X2?, Y3?))` to the log's; the monitor answers `time` in one reduction; `send_to_user` reduces on the entry, its guard `ground(T?)` walking the menu's two lists, and `_output` dereferences the term and formats it, two more walks, and prints the line, the two lists in it (`body_kernels.dart`, `outputKernel`, `formatGroundTerm`; `run.sh` then parses it).

The platform then reduces: the `agent` clause on `same(To)`, its guards `ground(Ss?), ground(Os?)`, two walks, writes `msg(To, offer(Id, same))` on its output stream, calls `ask` for the next menu, `agent` again, and `merge(D1?, D2?, D)` on the ask streams.  The message crosses `mwm_copy` and `stream_append` into the network stream, then log2 N `deliver` reductions down the tree to the addressee's input stream; the addressee's `agent` clause on `offer` calls `respond`, `answered`, `agent` and two `merge`s, one on the output streams, and the answer to the approval goes the same way: person process, `random`, `wary_approval`, `random`, `stream_append(rated(1/day, Tok))`, `wary_answer` on `Tok`, `relay_2`, `answer_2`, `time`, `entry`, `send_to_user`.  A yes costs three more messages, `accepted`, `friends`, `friends_back`, each through the tree, and `meet`, `add_all`, `insert`, which rebuild the known lists, O(n) reductions.

So one answer of the social graph is, counting the simulator's plumbing and the network and leaving the platform's own reductions aside: the person process 1, the two `random`s 2, the profile goal 1, the request's `stream_append` 1, the monitor's request clause about 5 plus log2 P, the release 1 plus log2 P, the pick 1, `one_of` about 1.5 n, the relay 1, the answer 1, the `time` round trip 2, the entry's `stream_append` 1, `send_to_user` 1, the network's `mwm_copy` 1 and `deliver` log2 N per message, with 1 to 4 messages per answer; and in Dart, nine walks of the known lists, each O(n), and the formatting and printing of the two lists.  About 20 plus 2 log2 P plus log2 N reductions and 1.5 n more, n the length of the agent's known list, against SpiFcp's three reductions and one C scan.

## 3. The deviations, each reviewed

Each item: SpiFcp's design, sGLP's, the cost as the code shows it, and whether it is justified by SpiFcp, by the paper, or by neither.

1. **Where the stochastic step runs.**  SpiFcp: in the emulator, in C, `spi_post`, `spi_step`, `spi_close`, called by the monitor through `execute`, with the same algorithm in FCP as the fallback when `spicomm` is not linked.  sGLP: wholly in GLP, the paper's Section 4: "The engine, the compiler and the type checker of GLP are not changed", "one thing from the machine, a guard".  Cost: the queue's insert and pop are GLP reductions, 2 log2 P an event, each allocating a node, about 20 reductions at 1,000 agents (P about 1,200: a menu per agent and the pending approvals), against a C scan with no allocation.  Justified by the paper's decision, not by SpiFcp, whose design put the step in the kernel.  A kernel step is an engine extension beyond the paper and needs Udi's approval; its FCP precedent is exact.

2. **What the events are.**  SpiFcp: the direct method over channels; a channel holds all the offers on it, its weight the base rate times the counts, so a thousand processes offering on one channel are one entry of the scan, and the C selection is per channel, then the first offer in the channel's queue (the oldest; a uniform one under `randomize`).  sGLP: the first reaction method with one pending event per rated goal, a heap of P entries.  The paper's Appendix "What sGLP does about them" says the grouping is not available because no two agents share a state.  Gillespie's selection needs the same rate, not the same state: the social graph's rated goals have four rates, so the pending goals fall into four classes, each with a count, and the direct method over the classes is O(1) an event, a class drawn by weight and a member drawn uniformly within it, exact by memorylessness; the paper's Proposition "Time to the Next Release" is the same proposition.  That is SpiFcp's channel, a rate class with a queue of offers, and in sGLP the class is the rate of the rated goal, known at the request.  It needs a set with O(1) insert, delete and uniform draw, an array, which GLP has not; so it is item 1's kernel.  Within a class SpiFcp's default takes the oldest offer, which is exact for the aggregate and not for an individual; for persons the draw is uniform.  Not justified by the paper's reason; justified by nothing once item 1 is settled.

3. **Idle detection.**  SpiFcp: `processor # machine(idle_wait(Wakeup))`, the kernel binding `Wakeup` once when idle.  sGLP: the guard `when_idle`, the scheduler waking the longest waiter once when its queue is empty (`wakeIdle`).  The same design; O(1); no deviation.

4. **The request transport.**  SpiFcp: the scheduler's FCP channel, `write_channel`.  sGLP: a mutual reference, `stream_append`, a kernel binding the tail in O(1).  The same design; no deviation.

5. **The clock.**  SpiFcp: a vector, `SPITIME`, read in place by any process, no message and no monitor reduction.  sGLP: a request `time(T)` appended to the monitor's stream and answered by a monitor reduction, one per log line, so two kernel appends and one reduction per answer, serialised through the monitor.  GLP has no cell a goal reads in place, so the request is forced by the language.  Not needed: the monitor knows the clock at the release, so the token can carry it, `go(T)` in place of `go`, and the released goal passes `T` to the answer; the `time` request then goes.  A change to the paper's `Token` type and its Section 4, small.

6. **The record.**  SpiFcp: per transmission `[Now, end(PName(RCId, arrow, CNId)), end(...)]`, names only, formatted after the run by `spi_record`.  sGLP: per answer the question's term with the answer in it, the paper's Definition "Log": for the social graph the menu with the agent's two known lists, up to N minus 1 peers, walked by `send_to_user`'s `ground`, dereferenced, formatted and printed, then parsed by `run.sh`'s awk.  Cost: O(n) Dart work and O(n) bytes a line, n growing through the year from 4 to about N minus 1; at 1,000 agents the menus' lines are the bulk of the run's output.  The paper's choice; not SpiFcp's.  Review: an entry could carry the answer and the question's identity, with what the person was shown logged once where it is shown rather than at every answer; for the social graph the menu's lists are the agent's known lists, which the log's own entries determine.  A change to Definition "Log".

7. **The network.**  SpiFcp: none; a send meets its receive in the channel's lists and the C transmission unifies the two messages.  sGLP: `mwm/2` over N output streams and a balanced tree of `deliver/4`, 1 plus log2 N reductions a message, 7 at 100 agents and 10 at 1,000, with 1 to 4 messages an answer; and a `merge/3` on an agent's output streams per pending approval.  The harness's design, by hand, for the paper's Cold-call.  GLP has no indexed delivery, so a tree is the best GLP can write.  Justified by GLP's semantics, since the deployed platform has a network where SpiFcp's processes share a store; the cost is a tenth to a quarter of an answer's plumbing.  A kernel routing by key, as `_send` routes in madGLP mode, would be O(1); an engine matter.

8. **The goals of a question.**  SpiFcp: one process suspended on `Chosen`, and one reduction of `.comm` when it is bound.  sGLP: the person process's clause, two `random`s, the profile goal, the relay, the pick goal, the answer goal, and the platform's `ask`, `ask1` and `merge`: about ten reductions and seven goals, the relay and the answer goal existing for Definition "Relay" and the soundness proof, the two `random`s for the seeds.  Justified by the paper's architecture, which SpiFcp's model of a process has no counterpart to; a review of the paper could fold the relay and the answer into the person process's clause where the question's type has one position the person writes, which both of the social graph's have.

9. **The draw of an alternative.**  SpiFcp: a sum posts all its offers in one request and C withdraws the losers (`discount`).  sGLP: the profile draws its branch before it waits, and one rated goal is pending.  Simpler, and cheaper; no cost.

10. **The heap against the linear queue.**  The approved deviation.  Its cost in GLP is item 1's: a skew heap written in GLP is 2 log2 P reductions an event, and each reduction allocates a node; SpiFcp's linear structure is a scan in C.  With item 1 settled the heap goes with the first reaction method, item 2, and a linear structure of rate classes takes its place; with item 1 kept, the heap stands.

11. **The draws.**  SpiFcp: C's `random()`, unseeded, so a run is not reproducible.  sGLP: `random/4` and `split_random_seed/3` threaded through the run, every seed split from the run's seed.  A design gain of sGLP's; cost one reduction per draw and, at setup, `skip(4096, B, B2)` per agent, 4,096 reductions an agent, 4 million at 1,000 agents, the paper's "about 2 s" at 100.  Setup only; justified.

12. **`ground` on the known lists.**  SpiFcp's processes carry channels, not lists.  sGLP walks the agent's known lists nine times an answer in Dart: `homophile_pick`'s guard once, `relay_1`'s twice, the `agent` clause's twice, `send_to_user`'s twice, and the dereference and the format of the entry.  The relay's and the log's are the transformation's and item 6's; the pick's is the profile's; the agent clause's are the platform's, graph.vglp's own text, which the phone would run too.  O(n) Dart each, cheap per element and growing through the year.

13. **`one_of`.**  The profile's choice of a peer: `length/2` then `nth/3`, about 1.5 n reductions, n the known list's length.  At 100 agents n reaches 97; at 1,000 it reaches 998, about 1,500 reductions a menu answer, more than all the plumbing together.  The paper's Section 5 says `one_of` is ordinary GLP; GLP lists have no O(1) index, and SpiFcp never indexes a list.  This, with items 6 and 12, is the growth through the year at 1,000 agents: the cost of an answer is linear in the number of people the agent knows, which the platform makes grow to N.

14. **`meet`, `add_all`, `insert`.**  The platform's own, O(n) reductions per yes, rebuilding the known and friend lists.  The simulated program's cost, which the phone pays once per agent and the simulator N times; not the simulator's.

## 4. What this says about the two measurements

The 2 ms an answer at 100 agents is the plumbing: about 20 reductions of the simulator and the network, plus 2 log2 P for the heap and log2 N per message for the tree, about 40 to 60 reductions, at the engine's tens of microseconds a reduction.  SpiFcp spends three reductions and one C scan there.  Items 1, 5, 7 and 8 are where the difference is, item 1 the largest.

The slowing through the year at 1,000 agents, 6 simulated months in the first ten minutes and half a month in the last, is items 13, 12 and 6: the cost of a menu answer is linear in the agent's known list, which grows from 4 toward 999 over the year; `one_of` alone is about 1,500 reductions a menu answer at the year's end, where the plumbing is about 60.  At 100 agents the list stops at 97 and the growth is hidden in the plumbing.  Item 7 adds a fixed factor, 10 against 7 reductions a message.

The memory, 927 MiB at the peak and growing through the year, is not determined by this reading.  The engine's heap cells are Dart objects, collected by Dart (`heap_fcp.dart`: `HP` is a serial counter, "No cell is found by it"), so no arena grows; the monitor's queue, the pending approvals and the live `merge` goals are bounded by the pending questions.  The one quantity that grows through the year is the allocation an answer makes, items 6, 12 and 13: the entry's term, its formatted line of up to a thousand peers, and `one_of`'s reductions.  Dart's collector lets the heap grow under a rising allocation rate before it collects, so the peak could be that; a measurement of the heap against the log's line length over the year would settle it, and this reading cannot.

## 5. What follows SpiFcp, in order

1. Item 1 with items 2 and 10: the monitor's step as a kernel of the engine, as `spicomm.c` is of the emulator, with the monitor in GLP kept as the fallback as `sum_weights` is in `spi_monitor.cp`; the pending goals in rate classes with counts, the direct method over the classes, a uniform draw within a class.  An engine extension beyond the paper: Udi's approval, then a change to the paper's Section 4 and Appendix C.
2. Item 5: the token carries the clock, `go(T)`; the `time` request goes.  A change to the paper's `Token` and Section 4.
3. Item 6: the log entry carries the answer and the question's identity, what the person was shown logged where it is shown.  A change to Definition "Log".
4. Item 13: the profile's draw over a list stays O(n) in GLP; the paper's example could keep the known list's length with the list, which halves it, and nothing in GLP makes `nth` O(1).  The platform's lists are the platform's.
5. Item 7: the network as a kernel routing by key.  An engine matter, after 1.
6. Item 8: folding the relay and the answer goal where the type has one position the person writes.  A paper review, lowest.
