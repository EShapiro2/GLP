## What the series says

**d.** A large d is what makes transitive pointing work.  At d = 3 the overlay is the friendships' (stretch 1.05–1.11 against the ideal of friends, 30 transitive edges of 121); at d = 8 a third of the edges are transitive (111–127 of 327) and the overlay is far below the ideal of friends (0.78), at 1.41–1.52 of the optimum against 1.52–1.58 at d = 3.  The price is the churn, three times the pointings made and ended, and a run twice as long.

**e.** At d = 3, e hardly matters (1.58 at 200 m, 1.52 at 2000 m).  At d = 8 a large e cuts the churn by a third (308 pointings made after the first day against 389) and lowers the stretch (1.41 against 1.52): with a small e a person re-points for a gain of a few hundred metres and the overlay chases the travellers; with a large one it keeps what it has unless a much nearer person appears.

**The town.** A hundred persons do worse than forty (1.92 against 1.52 at d = 3; 2.28 against 1.41 at d = 8, the hundred not yet converged at four days — 803 edges of 800, 24 persons above d, the overlay still forming): the optimum is shorter (976 m against 1541) while the friends, four nearest and two uniform, are as far as before, so the gap between friends and neighbours is wider.

**What a person knows at the start.** Knowing 8 nearest persons in place of 4 is the one knob of the series that moves the overlay towards the optimum: at d = 8 and e = 2000, from 1.41 to 1.31 at forty persons (72% of the pointings optimal, 7 in 10 of the optimum's edges) and from 2.28 to 1.61 at a hundred (61% optimal).  The friendships being more local, the ideal of friends falls (4418 → 3370 m at forty) and the overlay follows it down.

**The result.** The best overlay of the series, `t40_d8_e2000_near8`, is at 1.31 of the optimum with 72% of its edges optimal; the rest are at 1.4 to 2.3.  In every record the overlay reaches the ideal of friends or better (0.78–1.11), so what stands between it and the optimum is the friendship graph, not the instance: friendships made uniformly across the town (`Meet`) and the first-day friendships carried away by the travellers.  In no run does a pointing cross a component of the cumulative friendship graph (Proposition 2).

## To get a great result

Within GDO-LB as it stands and the regular behaviour: d = 8 or more, e large (2 km), persons knowing many neighbours at the start, and days enough for a large town to converge (the hundred needs more than four at d = 8).  Beyond it, the levers are the friendship graph's — whom a person meets (a neighbour, not a stranger drawn from the whole town) and how much the persons gossip — which are the persons' behaviour, not the contract's, and were not run here at Ohad's instruction to keep the regular behaviour; and the records of 2026-11-05 (in the history at `e3430fa7`) with the same finding.  A run of GDO-LB compiled to its own platform, the conditions enforced by the machine rather than chosen by the person, waits on GSC's term form and vGLP's environment predicates (`location`, `degree`, `threshold`).
