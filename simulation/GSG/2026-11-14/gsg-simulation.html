GSG, the Grassroots Social Graph, as the platform ran it

Runs of the GSG contract's canonical platform under sGLP, as recorded in cascade/GSG/2026-11-13. In the village runs the persons act as in CSSN's simulation, without the children: each reads what it holds and offers friendship to those it knows, introduces friends and ends friendships, and each answers offers by its own policy. In the other runs each person follows a script.

How to read it. GSG extends GKK, so a run has five acts. establish gives a person an identity, a key pair. acquaint is two persons meeting: each then knows the other. introduce is Alice telling Bob, who knows her, of Carol, whom she knows: Bob then knows Carol, one way, so two introductions are needed before Bob and Carol can become friends. befriend is the joint decision of two persons who know each other; a friendship is two halves, one held by each friend. unfriend is one friend's act and removes both halves. Each person holds only its own state: its identity, whom it knows and its friends; the table under the graph shows it. Each person, twice an hour, draws one proposal from its script; the platform refuses a proposal the state does not allow, and the other person is asked to approve. An agreeable person approves whatever it is asked; a choosy person, in the runs of twelve and more, declines one request in three, and a declined request changes nothing. In the village runs there is no script. The census, as in CSSN without the children, makes villages of ten households of one or two adults; each adult knows at the start the adults of their own village and two adults of other villages. Each adult first establishes an identity and meets those it knows; then, twice an hour on average, it reads its own view and acts on it: 45 times in 52 it offers friendship to someone it knows who is not yet a friend (in its own village 95 times in 100 if it is local, 60 if it is open), 6 times in 52 it introduces one friend to another, and once in 52 it ends a friendship. An offer is answered after half an hour on average: a keen adult accepts 90 times in 100, a choosy one 50. Each adult's profile is in the table. Times are simulated hours. The events are read from each person's own view and proposals in the run's log.
