# Claudese — do not use

Claude's stock words and phrases are banned: to Udi, to another Claude, and in every document and paper.  Write My English, Udi's own, the English of `writing-style-guide.md`.  Each entry is one line, `banned --- My English: <what to write>`; the text check, `tools/myenglish/check.py`, reads this form.  Udi adds more over time.

## Constructions

1. A mathematical object, a field, a definition, a section or a tool as the actor (a graph "decides", a literature "answers with", a field "asks how", a definition "makes it precise", "an unguarded schema signs nothing") --- My English: name who does what, with the plain verb: "the leader signs the message they deliver"
2. "not X but Y", and mirrored clauses ("separates what X does from what Y does") --- My English: state each side plainly
3. An elided verb ("its value the issuer's credit"; "its object the community's records, its configuration the assembly they compute") --- My English: write the verb: "its value being the issuer's credit"
4. A compressed clause ("the motion recording the assembly that decides it", "a supermajority of each assembly its motions record") --- My English: write each fact as a sentence
5. A collective noun for its members where the difference matters ("held by its assembly") --- My English: "every member of the assembly holds a copy"
6. A fronted participle, then a condition as the actor ("formalised as conditions on its acts, they give it the property he envisioned") --- My English: "a contract meeting these conditions has the property"
7. The cleft ("what is proposed here is their conjunction", "what bounds the coins is the central bank's issuance") --- My English: "Here we proposed their conjunction", "the central bank's issuance bounds the coins"
8. A bare possessive as predicate ("the distinction is Goodfriend's") --- My English: "Goodfriend draws the distinction"
9. A qualifier that repeats the sentence ("the coins alone create no credit, open no lending channel and add no instrument of policy") --- My English: "the coins neither create credit, nor open a lending channel, nor add an instrument of policy"; if it cannot be deleted the subject is wrong: "What the money component does not do"
10. An abstract noun as the subject of a negated verb ("The concept does not require this") --- My English: "That is a choice those designs made, not something a CBDC must be."
11. Our contribution written as a fault of others ("It is a limitation of these designs and not of the concept") --- My English: "Here we propose to augment the CBDC coins with CBDC bonds"
12. One claim restated in several registers --- My English: say it once, then why, then what follows
13. A sentence built of terms of art ("not denied legal effect for being electronic", "negotiable within the bills and notes acts") --- My English: the plain word in the body, the exact formulation in the citation: "legally binding also in electronic form"
14. A repair that adds a noun phrase ("and the three have yet to be measured") --- My English: "and have yet to be similarly measured"
15. "X is no Y" ("a sovereign grassroots coin is no fiat coin", "is no act of the contract") --- My English: "is not a fiat coin"; "no demand increases them" and "there is no X" stand
16. An invented name, a machine that speaks, an elided verb ("an else-step, by which the machine answers no when the person does not") --- My English: "an else-branch, which is taken when the person does not will the then-branch"
17. "proposes it whole", "the friend cannot pick the child" --- My English: say who must name whom: "Alice must name Bob's child Dave in the introduction"
18. An adjective of a relation before a plural noun ("have disjoint closures") --- My English: "the closures of the two transactions are disjoint", or "no step is in both closures"
19. A suffix listing what a quantifier covers ("by them or by a program", "whoever or whatever took it") --- My English: delete it: "accountable for every act signed with it"
20. A sentence saying what a definition excludes ("A child and an AI agent are not persons.") --- My English: delete it.  "When you define something, then EVERYTHING ELSE is excluded; if you exclude something specific, this may create the false impression that ONLY IT is excluded; therefore NEVER DO IT."
21. A negative restatement ("Guardianship ends by an act of a guardian and by no act of the ward") --- My English: "Guardianship ends by an act of a guardian."
22. A condition stated by what it rules out ("meeting may not depend on a number") --- My English: "Just say what can be": "every argument of an atom that meeting requires is one of the two persons who meet".  Before proposing a condition, check whether a definition already states it.
23. Sentences added to a one-line rule, a dash-list glossing "every", a sentence naming who breaks a rule --- My English: the rule, once
24. "If it has a cost, say what the cost is" --- My English: "If cost is very high, state it."
25. "Never leave it out because of its cost" --- My English: "NEVER NOT PROPOSE IT because of cost."
26. A question that lists the places and mechanics of a change ("say once in Section 4 that VMAT's person is our agent, use agent in the five places and in Section 9") --- My English: name what is decided, once: "so you follow the GAI terminology, and mention once the discrepancy with VMAT"
27. A recommendation that names edits and mechanisms ("I recommend the declaration decides: roles of different names are bound to different persons") --- My English: say what holds: "Alice and Bob are two persons, and Alice is one person, in a clause and in its schema."
28. A rule put by its mechanism and cases ("a name that the act cited does not have stands for the name of its kind of that act") --- My English: what holds, with one example: "a citation uses the names of its own contract, so that GO:4 writes Orga, the organisation, where GnW:2 writes Carol, the ward"
29. An ellipsis that can be read several ways ("and Carol and Alice to return them to Alice, as the agreed condition is met or fails") --- My English: say the verb each time and name the condition: "Carol and Alice may jointly decide to return them to Alice when it fails"
30. A claim in the vocabulary of form and content ("in Proudhon's substantive sense", "which are judged of what its clauses say, not of its schemas") --- My English: delete the claim
31. A tool or a text as the actor ("The compiler meets four things the pragmas and Section 5.4 cannot say") --- My English: "The pragmas and Section 5.4 do not say four things the apps need."
32. The present tense for what is not present ("she no longer trusts him", "by GSG:3 she ends the friendship") --- My English: "she can no longer trust him", "she must end the friendship".  "My English uses tenses AND modalities: present is only for things that are indeed in the present, including eternal.  EVERYTHING ELSE is either past or future, may or must, possibly or necessarily, etc., but not present."
33. A rule written as a description ("last, alone, marked Q: or Request:, and nothing after it"; "sentences only, no write of any kind") --- My English: an instruction in plain verbs, once: "comes last"; "No writing"
34. A vague verb for what was run ("I took the gate as its sections run separately on one commit") --- My English: "Before merging into main I did not run the whole suite in one go.  I ran each of its parts by itself, all on the same commit, and merged when every part had passed."
35. An abstract noun as subject and the paper's machinery for what happens ("A change of a fact the phone finds is a trigger of the agent, read with its other triggers.") --- My English: "When a fact Alice's phone finds changes, as when a person comes near, the phone tells Alice's agent, which then checks again which of its own transactions it can take."

## The rule under them

**Think of Udi as the reader**: "Why don't you use this clear language in the paper, only with me?  think of me as the reader".  The sentence you would write to him in chat is the sentence for the paper.  A paragraph has three sentences: the claim, its warrant, the proposal.
