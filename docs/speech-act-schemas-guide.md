# Rendering a Grassroots Contract as Speech-Act Schemas --- a Guide

An evolving guide, written by GSC #7 Cowork at Udi's request (2026-10-24), held in the GLP repository's `docs/` since 2026-11-01 at his word, so that every contract writer sees it; brought current with the paper by GSC #13 (2026-11-07).  It defines nothing: speech-act schemas, bindings, their denotation and the three conditions are defined in the GSC paper, `/Grassroots/GSC/main.tex`, Section 3, the relations among contracts in Section 8, the modalities in Section 9 and values in the appendix Values in Speech-Act Schemas, and where this guide and the paper differ, the paper is right and this guide is fixed.  A schema is a first-order universally-quantified implication, `Head <- Body guarded by Alice, Bob`, the holder of every atom its first argument, the body what the act requires of the persons' machines, a negated atom in it what the act forbids, the head what the act adds, a negated atom in it what it deletes, a deleted atom standing in the body too (Udi, 2026-11-04, to GSC #11).  GSG:1 reads `befriend: friend(Alice, Bob), friend(Bob, Alice) <- knows(Alice, Bob), not friend(Alice, Bob), knows(Bob, Alice), not friend(Bob, Alice) guarded by Alice, Bob`.  The guide holds the method, the rules of practice and examples, good and bad, each bad one a fault found and corrected.  Add to it whenever a contract teaches something.  The term form a contract is written in, `sas(Name, Names, Vars, Head, Body, Constraint, Guards)`, is Code's README, `programs/gsc/README.md`, "Writing a contract"; how a contract reaches the repository is `GLP/docs/writing-a-contract.md`; how a paper prints a contract and its schemas is the style guide, `/Grassroots/docs/writing-style-guide.md`, 36 and 40.

## The one rule

The schemas capture the contract: each act a clause describes is a schema, and each schema says what its clause says, no more and no less.  A schema is judged by whether it renders its clause, never by whether it allows the right acts: two schemas can allow the same acts and one of them still misrender the clause.

## Method

1. Read the clause and list the acts it describes.  A clause that describes no act --- an accountability, a personal acquaintance, a price kept --- gets no schema; its legal modality says what the realisation does with it (GSC, Section 9), and an undertaken clause may say what its obligor undertakes as a formula over runs, `phi leads-to psi` (GSC, Definition Undertaking formula; Udi, 2026-11-05).
2. Name the persons from the clause's own names, Alice first, in the order the clause names them; a person who holds an atom of the clause is a role of the schema, and one who is only named, as Carol in GKK:3, a variable.
3. Write the guard from the clause's words: "may decide" one person, "may jointly decide" and "by an act of both" each named, "by their own act" the one.  A person the clause does not make will the act is not a guard; an act no one wills, such as delivery, has no guard.  Any agent variable of the schema may guard, a listed role among them, every member then a guard (Udi, 2026-11-06).
4. Write the body, what the clause requires of each person's machine, `knows(Alice, Bob)`, and forbids, `not friend(Alice, Bob)`; and the head, what it adds, `friend(Alice, Bob)`, and deletes, `not friend(Alice, Bob)`, a deleted atom also in the body.  Take each phrase of the clause and write it as an atom whose holder is the person it is about.  A phrase about every person another person lists, or a condition on what one person holds, is a quantified conjunct; a phrase about amounts is a multiplicity or a constraint (the rules of practice below).
5. Read the clause back as English, person by person, and compare it with the contract's clause phrase by phrase.  Every phrase of the clause must be in the schema, and every atom of the schema must come from a phrase of the clause or from a clause the contract extends.
6. Run the checker (`programs/gsc`, task to Code) and read its verdict against the paper's three conditions.  A failing condition is a fault in the rendering or in the clause, never a reason to bend the schema.  A contract written from a certified one is checked by what Theorem What the check preserves says (GSC, Section 8): an amendment by its added schemas and the introductory act, a strengthening of bodies by the introductory act and any reading role it adds, an instance of a parametric contract not at all.
7. Write the pragmas: a line per tab, naming it and what it lists; then, for each clause, a line naming it, the person who proposes, a button for each act they propose, bearing the act's name, and any field they fill in angle brackets; an indented line for each other proposer, written the same way; and an indented line for each other person asked, their name, a colon, the question (GSC, Section 2; Udi, 2026-10-25).  As GKK's Acquaintance: `Acquaintance: Alice [Acquaint] <person>` and, indented, `Bob: Alice wishes that you know each other.`  A button is offered for each way the proposer's own state satisfies the act's atoms at the proposer, and a field is for a variable of the act those atoms do not bind, a text, a number, a new name, a person not yet known; so Acquaintance has a field and Introduction, Join and Remove none (vGLP #23's proposal, Udi, 2026-11-05).  A question line belongs to the nearest button line above it: where a proposer's acts ask questions, each act takes a line, as GAI's `Alice [Agent meet]` then `Carol: ...`, then `Alice [Agent befriend]` then `Carol: ...`; buttons share a line only when no question follows (Udi, 2026-10-25).  A name in a pragma is the schema's role of that name: name the schema's roles as the clause does, Alice, Bob, Carol (Udi, 2026-10-25).
8. Open the contract as the paper's boxes do.  The first line, alone, states its relation to the contract it is written from, in Section 8's terms and with its verb, `GSN amends GSG.`, `GSG extends GKK.` (Udi, 2026-11-06); then the declaration of its names, `Alice, Bob and Carol are persons.`, which the schemas and the pragmas take their names from; a contract whose names differ in kind says so there, as `Alice and Bob are adults, Carol and Dave children.` (Udi, 2026-10-25, on CSSN's proposal).  A person takes the pronoun `they`; for readability a clause may write `she` for Alice and `he` for Bob etc. (Udi, 2026-11-07, to GSC #13).  A kind other than person is a fact in the agent's state, an atom like any other, which the schemas require or forbid: a contract with wards replaces GKK's introduce with its own, in which Carol, a person by GKK's declaration, is a reading role that does not hold the ward's atom (Udi, 2026-11-03, to GSC #10).  Two names are two persons; a clause that admits one person in both writes `Alice and Bob, possibly one person`, and the schema's head writes the second name as `one('Bob', 'Alice')`, admitted only where neither role adds or deletes an atom naming the other (Udi, 2026-10-25, on CSSN's proposal, for siblings under one parent).  A schema may require an atom of an environment predicate, which the platform keeps current and no schema adds or deletes, as GB's today (GSC, Appendix Values in Speech-Act Schemas).

## Rules of practice

- **Put each requirement at the person it is about.**  "her friend Bob" is about Alice's friends, so friend(Alice, Bob), Alice the holder.
- **Add no requirement the clause does not state.**  A requirement that holds anyway, because another clause keeps it true, is still not the clause's.
- **The extended contract's clauses come whole.**  Every role but establish's requires identity (GKK:1); a contract that extends GKK requires it without restating the clause.  An amendment that changes an act of the other contract replaces it, keeping no act beside its replacement: GSN's unfriend replaces GSG's (GSC, Section 2).  Which relation a contract has to another --- amendment, extension, instantiation, implementation, restriction --- is Section 8's to say, and the contract's first line says which.
- **A clause whose acts differ in form becomes several schemas**, named by the act and a suffix, as swap_cc and swap_bc of one [Swap] button; the clause and its button stay one.
- **Say what can be, never what is excluded** (claudese.md): a condition, a precondition or a forbidden atom states what must hold; a list of what is ruled out is never complete.
- **No new construct for convenience.**  The guard is the one mode; a construct enters the language only when the paper says why (Udi, 2026-10-18).  The syntax is first-order universally-quantified implications over multisets of atoms, with a guard, the body a safe formula, which with cardinality is first-order logic with counting and with sum one that also sums, and nothing else; the wildcard, the listed role and the multiplicity are its notation (GSC, Section 3.2).  The paper restricts this syntax only where a proof uses the restriction, and says which; a restriction no proof needs is removed (Udi, 2026-11-06).
- **The domain is multisets.**  A machine's state is a multiset of atoms, and the semantics reads it as a structure over multisets: an atom with multiplicity k holds where the machine holds k copies or more, its negation where fewer; a quantifier ranges over the tuples the machine holds; a count or a sum takes each instance as many times as its multiplicity (GSC, Section 3.1, Definition Extent; Udi, 2026-11-06).
- **Representation lies beneath the schemas.**  How a holding is split into atoms is the realisation's, not a clause's act (Udi, 2026-10-24).
- **The wildcard is an anonymous variable, anywhere.**  In an unnegated atom `_` is a variable of its own, one per occurrence; in a negated atom it is universally quantified over its argument, the atom forbidding, in the body, and deleting, in the head, every atom that matches it (GSC, Definition Speech-act schema; Udi, 2026-11-04, 2026-11-06).
- **A cleared atom deletes every copy of a form.**  A negated atom of the head with the wildcard `_` among its arguments, or for the speech act of a name term, is cleared: it deletes every atom of the machine's state it matches and requires none (Udi, 2026-10-30).  Use it where a clause ends every relation of a kind at once: GSN's unfriend has `not listed(Alice, Bob, _)` and `not member(Alice, <Bob>._)` in its head, so a friendship ending ends every membership between the two.  A cleared atom puts no edge in the role graph, being required nowhere, and counts as no change of state.
- **A quantified conjunct is `forall Xs (A and phi -> psi)`**, over every value of its variables, of any kind, at which an agent role holds the anchor `A` and `phi` holds; in the body `psi` must hold, in the head it is made to hold (GSC, Definition Safe formula, name term, role).  Where the bound variable is a person holding literals, as closing a group reaches every member the leader lists, the body carries `forall Bob (listed(Alice, Bob, n) -> member(Bob, <Alice>.n))`, what every such Bob must hold, and the head `forall Bob (listed(Alice, Bob, n) -> not member(Bob, <Alice>.n))`, what changes at each; the quantified person is a listed role, written Alice[listed(_, n)] in the paper, its anchor held by its base, and a listed role may guard (Udi, 2026-11-04, 2026-11-06).  Where no bound variable is a person holding literals, the conjunct is a condition on one person's own state and makes no listed role, as a ride-hailing contract awards a ride to Bob only if every bid Alice holds scores no more than Bob's; a literal an agent role holds in a conjunct of the head is conditional, added or deleted at each instance (GSC, Definition Speech-act schema).  So a schema is an implication and not a clause: the full power of universally quantified first-order logic is used.
- **A body is a safe formula.**  Built from atoms and constraints by `and`, `or`, `not` and the anchored quantifiers `exists Xs (A and phi)` and `forall Xs (A and phi -> psi)`, every bound variable among the arguments of its anchor, so it is evaluated over the machines' finite states; a head is literals and quantified conjuncts (GSC, Definition Safe formula, name term, role; Udi, 2026-11-06).  The three conditions read of a formula only the listed roles it binds and, for Provenance, the anchor of a conjunct as an atom its holder requires.
- **Provenance reads a safe formula too.**  A name an act adds must be traceable: the base of every argument of an added atom is the base of a role, or of an argument of an atom an agent role requires, or is named by a safe formula of the body, an atom of it held by an agent role naming the base in every disjunct, or the constraint `u = v` beside an atom naming `v` (GSC, Definition Traceable provenance; GFF #4's proposal, Udi supporting, 2026-11-07).  So `or(lpoints_to(Bob, Carol, Dave), and(friend(Bob, Carol), eq(Carol, Dave)))` at Bob traces Dave.
- **A reading role is a condition held by a third person.**  A person who holds atoms of the body and none of the head, and is not a guard, is a reading role: they neither will the act nor change state, and their machine's state is a condition of the act, read by the others.  In a schema with a guarding role, Volition admits one only where a guarding role requires an atom naming it, as Alice, who knows Bob, may act on what Bob holds, or where it is a listed role whose base is a guarding role, its anchor naming it (GSC, Definitions Speech-act schema and Volition; Udi, 2026-11-02, 2026-11-06).  Use it where a clause makes an act conditional on what a person who is not party to it holds, as GDO:2's Bob; clause 2 of Unobstructed admits no reading role for the other of the two introduced.
- **Every so often is an act no one wills.**  A clause saying an act is taken whenever it can be is rendered by a schema with no guard: VMAT's liveness takes an act that stays enabled (GSC, Section 5; Udi, 2026-11-03).  Such a schema may have reading roles, agent roles only, provided its role graph is connected and it has signature atoms: atoms added or deleted at one agent role, each the only one of its predicate the schema adds (deletes or clears) and of a predicate no other schema with no guarding role adds (deletes or clears), together naming every other agent role of the schema (GSC, Definitions Signature atoms and Volition; Udi, 2026-11-06).  GFF:2's delivery, "Alice, who follows Carol's feed through Bob, receives from Bob every item of Carol's feed that Bob holds": Bob reads, Alice adds the item and the record that it was received from Bob, the signature atom.
- **A constraint is any decidable condition on the values.**  Written in the body, after its atoms, in the relations and functions the values' types provide, the order and arithmetic of numbers, the distance of points, combined by the connectives; every binding satisfies it and no condition reads it, the realisation evaluating it when it binds the schema (GSC, Definition Schema with values; Udi, 2026-11-02, 2026-11-06).  It may also hold `u = v` and `u != v` of two agent variables, two speech-act variables or two name terms (GSC #12, 2026-11-06); `cardinality(Xs, A and phi)`, the number of instances of the anchor at which phi holds, each counted by its multiplicity, as `cardinality(x, points(Alice, x)) < d`; and `sum(Xs, A and phi, u)`, a numeric term summed over those instances, as BCC's backing (Udi, 2026-11-06).  An introductory act, and an arity-one act of clause 1 of Unobstructed, carry none and have literals for body and head.
- **Signing conditions.**  In a constraint, `signed(u, y)` says that the person in `u` signed the speech act `y` or a speech act inside it, and `unsigned(u, y)` that they did not (GSC, Definition Binding with values; Udi, 2026-11-01, on GFF's proposal).  No condition reads them.
- **A multiplicity counts copies.**  `k.A` in the head adds or deletes k copies, in the body requires k, and negated in the body forbids k, the machine then holding fewer; k a positive whole number or a value variable (GSC, Definition Schema with values).  A deleted atom is required: where an atom is required k times and deleted with multiplicities j_1, ..., j_n, the constraint must imply j_1 + ... + j_n <= k, which the checker decides where the constraint is linear over whole numbers and refuses otherwise (GSC #11, 2026-11-06).  A body reads the state before the act, so "stays backed" is written as the condition on it that the head makes equivalent (Udi, 2026-11-06).
- **Implementable contracts.**  vGLP's compiler realises a schema with no guard only where one machine's state names the whole act: a contract is implementable if, in each schema with no guarding role, the atoms its first role requires name every other role and every variable of what it adds and deletes (GSC, Section 8).  A contract's abstraction level is not restricted by it: write the clause as the contract says it, and an implementable contract implements or restricts it, as GFF-I does GFF (GFF #3's proposal, Udi, 2026-11-06).
- **One modality a clause.**  A clause takes one (GSC, Section 9), and a clause whose parts differ takes the weakest (Udi, 2026-10-23, reported by CSSN #10).

## Good examples

- **befriend**, GSG:1, "Alice and Bob, who trust each other and are not friends, may jointly decide to become friends": `friend(Alice, Bob), friend(Bob, Alice) <- knows(Alice, Bob), not friend(Alice, Bob), knows(Bob, Alice), not friend(Bob, Alice) guarded by Alice, Bob`; "jointly decide" is the guard, "trust each other" the two knows atoms, trust presupposing that each knows the other, "are not friends" the two negated ones, "become friends" the head.  The rest of the trust describes no act, and the clause is attested; GSG:3, that Alice ends the friendship when she ceases to trust Bob, has no schema and is undertaken.
- **unfriend**, GSG:2, "Alice may end her friendship with Bob": `not friend(Alice, Bob), not friend(Bob, Alice) <- friend(Alice, Bob), friend(Bob, Alice) guarded by Alice`; the friendship deleted at both, so a friendship never ends for one person only.
- **introduce**, GKK:3: `knows(Bob, Carol) <- knows(Alice, Bob), knows(Alice, Carol), knows(Bob, Alice) guarded by Alice, Bob`; Carol holds nothing and is a variable.
- **close**, GSN:2, "Alice may close the group by leaving it": `closed(Alice, n), not listed(Alice, Alice, n), not member(Alice, <Alice>.n), forall Bob (listed(Alice, Bob, n) -> not member(Bob, <Alice>.n)) <- member(Alice, <Alice>.n), listed(Alice, Alice, n), forall Bob (listed(Alice, Bob, n) -> member(Bob, <Alice>.n)) guarded by Alice`; one act of the leader and every member the leader lists, so no member is left out.
- **deliver**, GSN:4, "its leader delivers it to every current member": `delivered(Alice, x, Bob, n), received_in(Bob, x, Alice, n) <- member(Alice, <Alice>.n), posted(Alice, x, n), listed(Alice, Bob, n), friend(Alice, Bob), not delivered(Alice, x, Bob, n), member(Bob, <Alice>.n)`, no guard: the leader undertook it on creating the group, and liveness takes it; one act per member.

## Bad examples, found and fixed

- **message**, GSN:1, "Alice may send a message to her friend Bob" (fixed 2026-10-24, on vGLP #20's finding).  The schema required friend(Bob, Alice) and nothing of Alice.  It allowed the right acts, friendships being held at both sides, but it said that Alice is Bob's friend where the clause says Bob is Alice's.  Fixed: friend(Alice, Bob), nothing of friendship at Bob.  Lesson: the first rule and the first rule of practice.
- **join**, GSN:3, "Alice, the leader of a group, may add her friend Bob to it": the schema also required friend(Bob, Alice), which no clause says (fixed the same evening).  Lesson: add no requirement the clause does not state.
- **dated coins**: GB's term wrote a coin with a date, cent(u, d).  A dated instrument is a bond; a coin has no date (Udi, 2026-10-24).  Lesson: the predicates carry the contract's own words for its things.
- **"an unguarded schema signs nothing"** (2026-10-18): a schema does not sign; a person does.  Lesson: name the person in every sentence about a schema.
- **a restriction no proof needs** (2026-11-06): the paper once asked that a listed role never guard, that a head's variables occur in the body, that a signature atom name every variable, that a constraint be linear; a contract of GRH's and one of GFF's could not be written under them, and no proof used them.  Lesson: a clause is written as the contract says it; a restriction of the language that no proof needs is removed and reported, not asked (GSC's CLAUDE.md).

## Pitfalls

- Checking a schema by its runs.  The runs can be right and the rendering wrong; compare phrase by phrase (Method 5).
- Copying a schema from a contract that extends or is extended: a copy keeps the fault of the original.  When a schema of GSN is fixed, CSSN's and GAI's copies follow, and the owner tells them.
- Putting a person in the guard because the act is "important to" them.  A person is a guard when the clause makes them will the act.
- An act that relates a person to themselves through two roles: a binding sends two roles to one agent only when the clause admits them as possibly one and neither names the other (GSC, Definition Binding).
- Numbers in conditions: a value names no agent and has no base, so the conditions do not read it (GSC, Appendix Values); a multiplicity counts copies and is a positive whole number.
- Deleting an unguarded schema, or strengthening its body without an invariant: either may drop an act the original owes, and is no restriction (GSC, Section 8).
- Whole against fractional coins and typed speech-act schemas are Currencies' to resolve (Udi, 2026-10-24); until then the examples take coins whole.

## Owners

The contract's owner writes its schemas, from its paper, and tasks Code to check and run them (claude.md, "Sessions").  This guide is for every owner; GSC answers questions on it, the speech-act-schema language being GSC's.
